# Set 9 · Improvement round 2

The critiques describe the round 1 output. I inspected each 480 × 460 and 640 × 480 image side by side at native size. The narrower viewport exaggerates the height of circles and shallow angles in several compositions.

## Critiques and priorities

### 146 · Paper Thermal

The wide wings make a bold upward gesture. Lemon against violet has excellent contrast, but the flat gallery surfaces give the bird and shadow little sense of place. The angular facets are a natural use of filled NAPLPS polygons. Folded sculpture is a distinct choice for the set, though the tall wing partly swallows the head and makes the silhouette read as an abstract zigzag. “PAPER THERMAL” is clear at 480 × 460.

Priorities are to separate the head from the wing, show the smaller folds that distinguish paper from a flat emblem, and give the projection a softer edge on a lit gallery surface.

### 147 · Soap Film

The tilted frame is striking, and the colour order works, but the similarly spaced stripes look printed on a solid panel. The bright bands peek around the left wire in both renders. Contiguous fills suit the medium and the optical subject is original within the set, provided the liquid is visible. “SOAP FILM” is clear at 480 × 460.

Priorities are to vary the drainage contours and add tiny thinning patches, and to show the joined wire and a wet lower meniscus with a hanging drop.

### 148 · Final Spin

The mint drum dominates three readable bays, but the broad pavement wedge looks like a rug and the pink line reads as an unfinished sign rule. The circular openings and palette blink suit the medium. A lone running machine gives the storefront idea a distinctive narrative within the set. The side drums are empty black disks, and the active cloth forms a nearly symmetrical propeller. “FINAL SPIN” is readable at 480 × 460, with thinner strokes than the other titles.

Priorities are to build depth into the bays and drum doors, and to replace the pavement wedge with broken reflections on irregular wet paving.

### 149 · Rabbit in the Wall

The hand and rabbit make a clean left-to-right read. Amber against near black sells the trick, but the spotlight is still an obvious stack of rings and the rabbit’s isolated bust floats. The silhouette contrast and native light shapes suit NAPLPS. The pairing of gesture and shadow is more original than either figure alone. Pointed fingers and ears look roughly cut at either size. “RABBIT IN THE WALL” is clear at 480 × 460.

Priorities are to throw the light across an actual wall, smooth the gesture and rabbit outlines, and connect the shadow to a projected wrist at the beam edge.

### 150 · Wind Organ

The rising pipe row reads against the chartreuse, and the dark lower terrain gives it a strong base. Dashed arcs and repeated extrusions use NAPLPS well, but the arrows read as diagram notation, the sockets look like small platforms, and the pipes are still closer to chimney stacks than to an instrument. Wind-powered sculpture is a distinctive subject. Both “WIND” and “ORGAN” read at 480 × 460.

Priorities are to add instrument-specific fittings and a shared anchoring structure, and to give the cliff face strata and the incoming air visible movement.

### 151 · Kiln Opening

The centred brick arch frames the tall bottle well, but the uniform orange stipple flattens the back wall and all three pots are shaded by similar straight splits. The warm reveal makes good use of WAIT and palette animation. The ceremonial focus on fired pottery is specific to this page, though the static composition still reads as an ordinary display alcove. “KILN OPENING” is clear at 480 × 460.

Priorities are to show heat and chamber recession with a lit back wall and hearth, and to give each vessel profile-following ridges and distinct glaze marks.

### 152 · Pocket Universe

The off-centre cluster is strong, but the similarly opaque stripes and identical glint pattern make the marbles look like striped sweets. The hard shadow ellipses reinforce that impression. Clipped polygons and arcs do credible optical work already. Glass overlaps earlier material studies, so the specific tabletop lens effect is the original part. “POCKET UNIVERSE” is clear at 480 × 460.

Priorities are to add a second tabletop pattern that bends together with the stripes, and to use contact shadows, coloured caustics and window reflections to sell the glass.

### 153 · Lock Within

The orange field and ivory caps separate the parts well. Exploded layers and dotted assembly axes suit NAPLPS. The pin stacks distinguish this subject from earlier diagrams, but the zigzags look schematic and the rectangular cavity terminates in a keyhole icon that explains little. Both “LOCK WITHIN” and “FOUR PIN STUDY” are readable at 480 × 460.

Priorities are to draw helical springs and profile the pins, and to show a cylindrical plug passing through the cutaway with a visible shear line.

### 154 · Two on the Ice

The red and blue skaters balance each other, but the loops are equally crisp along their full length and look drawn on a sheet. The pale angular banks and scattered dashes do little to identify ice. Polylines are a good fit for the crossing trajectories, and the overhead duet remains distinct from the other subjects. The stiff limbs read like pinwheels at 480 × 460. “TWO ON THE ICE” is clear there.

Priorities are to roughen the ice with directional scores and taper the older cuts, and to differentiate the skaters’ body positions and show fresh spray where blades meet ice.

### 155 · Updraft of Umbrellas

The coral lead canopy and receding flock make a striking diagonal. Rotated polygons and hook arcs suit the medium and the runaway flock is an original treatment of weather. The broad hard-edged ribbons look more solid than air, and the inverted canopy looks like a small boat because the exposed frame is missing. “UPDRAFT OF” and “UMBRELLAS” are both legible at 480 × 460.

Priorities are to break the gusts into uneven filaments and separate rain from airflow, and to articulate the canopy ribs, especially the exposed mechanism on the inverted umbrella.
