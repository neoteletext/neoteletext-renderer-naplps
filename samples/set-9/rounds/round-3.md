# Set 9 · Improvement round 3

## Critiques and priorities

These critiques describe the starting renders, inspected at 480 × 460 and 640 × 480. The narrower image stretches rounded forms vertically relative to the wider image.

### 146 · Paper Thermal

Lemon on violet gives the angular bird a decisive silhouette, and layered NAPLPS polygons make the folds economical and sharp. Folded paper in flight is distinct within the set, but the head still reads as a third wing and the shadow floats inside rings that look like a rug. “PAPER THERMAL” is clear at 480 × 460. Give the bird a recognizable head and beak, and make the light and shadow share a coherent floor plane.

### 147 · Soap Film

The oblique frame and black surround give the saturated bands room. The contour fills play to NAPLPS, and soap-film interference is a distinctive optical subject, but the spectrum looks too even and the black islands look printed on a rigid surface. The straight, matte wire also undercuts the single crude-looking drip. “SOAP FILM” is clear at 480 × 460. Twist the drainage contours and add an edge-fed wet fringe with a curved specular trace.

### 148 · Final Spin

The storefront reads frontally and the mint door rightly wins over the low-contrast violet bays. Circular doors and palette blink give the active load a role that suits the medium. The last load in an empty shop is a specific narrative, but the three bays look like copies, the side drums are effectively holes, and the sign rule at right still looks unfinished. “FINAL SPIN” is clear at 480 × 460. Render glass and an interior behind the machines, and give the sign and pavement a shared light source.

### 149 · Rabbit in the Wall

The warm hand and near-black rabbit make the transformation immediate. Filled silhouettes give the trick good graphic economy, and the shadow-play pairing fits the brief better than a standalone rabbit portrait would. The tiered light reads as a target, and the isolated shadow wrist ends in a conspicuous dark lump. “RABBIT IN THE WALL” is readable at 480 × 460. Break up the spotlight steps through plaster texture and carry the projected wrist toward the frame edge with a clearer penumbra.

### 150 · Wind Organ

The terracotta and chartreuse separate well, and the repeating bore ellipses give six distinct silhouettes and pitches. The instrument is an original landscape subject, but the repeated airborne arcs resemble fountains and the cliff is a broad, dark slab. “WIND” and “ORGAN” are clear at 480 × 460. Make a continuous crosswind interact with the pipes, and articulate the exposed rock face and its connection to the mount.

### 151 · Kiln Opening

The symmetrical arch usefully corrals the vessels, and orange against the plum masonry makes the reveal strong. WAIT and palette updates are especially apt for a sudden opening. The fired-ware scene is distinct, but the unbroken refractory arcs look like a rainbow, and the narrow straight shade on the vessels leaves them flat. “KILN OPENING” is clear at 480 × 460. Turn the arch into visibly jointed refractory courses and model each pot with a wider curved shoulder light and a lip with depth.

### 152 · Pocket Universe

The triangle of three marbles is balanced and readable against apricot, and the warped grid takes advantage of clipped polygon fills. Refraction distinguishes the study from a generic glass still life, but the heavy outlines and repeated four-square glints still make the spheres look like sweets with stickers. The 480 × 460 view also exaggerates their apparent oval shape. “POCKET UNIVERSE” is clear at channel size. Add a finer pattern that travels into each sphere, and vary the reflections and caustics with sphere position and curvature.

### 153 · Lock Within

Ivory components read well on orange, and the axes make the exploded NAPLPS construction easy to follow. The mechanism is a distinctive subject, though the assembled block is shallow and the shear line dissolves into the horizontal framing. The springs read as piles of stacked washers. “LOCK WITHIN” and “FOUR PIN STUDY” are both clear at 480 × 460. Draw continuous helices and make the junction of the plug and pins the focus of the cutaway.

### 154 · Two on the Ice

The high-key blue field and red and blue figures balance the frame. Long polylines make good blade paths, and an overhead skating duet remains a distinct choice. The skaters still look like little crossed limbs, and the uniformly pale crossings hide which cut lies on top. “TWO ON THE ICE” is clear at 480 × 460. Make the two poses and their near-blade shadows more distinct, and differentiate the active cuts where the paths intersect.

### 155 · Updraft of Umbrellas

The lead canopy commands the diagonal, and the coral and lilac accents are clear on ink green. Transformed polygon panels and native handle arcs make effective use of NAPLPS, and runaway umbrellas are an inventive storm subject. The thick, parallel gust bands still feel solid, and the inverted canopy’s cramped mechanism looks like an unfinished boat interior. “UPDRAFT OF” and “UMBRELLAS” are both readable at 480 × 460. Replace the broad bands with diverging airflow and give the upturned canopy a clearer exposed runner, ribs and stretchers.
