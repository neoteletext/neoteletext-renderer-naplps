# Set 9 · Improvement round 1

The critique describes the renders before editing. Each pair was viewed side by side at native 480 × 460 and 640 × 480. Byte counts refer to NAPLPS streams. Earlier sets were checked for subject overlap.

## Critiques and priorities

### 146 · Paper Thermal · 571 bytes

The bright off-centre wings against violet make a clean, readable silhouette at either size. The strong facets and connected crease use very few bytes and suit folded paper. The crafted bird distinguishes it from earlier bird subjects. The head reads as a sideways winglet and the low-contrast shadow resembles a second, differently posed bird. At 480 × 460, “PAPER THERMAL” is clear.

Priorities: give the bird a folded neck and beak, and derive the shadow from the same silhouette so the two shapes agree.

### 147 · Salt Bloom · 2,733 bytes

The warm central hopper anchors the cool satellites inside the dark field, although the circles elongate at 480 and the overlap hides two of the smaller hollows. Nested fills and logical pels work well, but the particles cost a fair share of the stream for what looks like scattered noise. The terraces resemble stacked squares more than irregular crystals. “SALT” and “BLOOM” both read at 480, with “BLOOM” close to the bottom. Set 8 already explores salt crystals, the clearest subject repeat in this set.

Priorities: replace the salt study with a stretched soap film in an angular wire frame, and use contiguous interference contours to show the film thinning.

### 148 · Final Spin · 842 bytes

The three bays read immediately and the mint centre attracts the eye through the muted violet façade. Native circles and a local palette blink make economical machinery, although the sparse marks around the cloth read more as a symbol than as a spinning load. The hard floating trapezoid and four horizontal dashes barely suggest wet pavement. The closed laundromat is distinctive even among earlier night storefronts. “FINAL SPIN” is clear at 480.

Priorities: show the laundry sweeping around the drum and align reflected light with its sources on the façade.

### 149 · Rabbit in the Wall · 646 bytes

The hand-to-rabbit diagonal is strong and the warm beam makes the dark shadow instantly readable. Native circles do the work cheaply, but the evenly spaced high-contrast rings look like a target. Both hands of the illusion end in hard, angular cuts, especially the rabbit’s flat, bust-like base. The idea differs from the earlier fox and cat silhouettes because the hand supplies the cause. “RABBIT IN THE WALL” reads clearly at 480, although the light nearly touches the tops of the last letters.

Priorities: soften the spotlight edge and shorten it above the title, and shape the shoulder and fingers to clarify how the live gesture becomes the shadow.

### 150 · Wind Organ · 1,442 bytes

Terracotta separates well from the bright chartreuse sky and the dark ground. Both “WIND” and “ORGAN” are clear at 480. Extruded bodies and native dashed arcs are efficient, but identical pipe widths and long straight faces make the row resemble chimneys. The airborne dashed marks stop well clear of the mouths and the bases look pasted onto flat bands. A wind-played instrument is an unusual subject even though the layered hills recall earlier landscapes.

Priorities: vary the pipes and seat them in the cliff with cast shadows, and route the airflow through identifiable pipe mouths.

### 151 · Kiln Opening · 2,440 bytes

The arch centres the eye, and orange heat against the muted brick sells the reveal. Native filled arcs and WAIT with palette changes give the page a reason to be a NAPLPS stream. The aligned, nearly identical pots and uniform crosshatch make it resemble a flat display niche. Firing ceramics is distinctive despite the recurring arch silhouette across the sample collection. “KILN OPENING” reads at 480.

Priorities: give each vessel a different throwing profile and glaze, and use arch masonry and a lighted hearth to give the chamber depth.

### 152 · Pocket Universe · 2,090 bytes

The triangular arrangement and white glints give the still life a strong read. The peach stripe field is louder than the shadows, and each sphere carries the same opaque-looking squiggle. Filled circles and arcs efficiently give it a glass vocabulary, but the refraction never shows a relationship to the stripe immediately outside a marble. Glass spheres recall earlier glass and orbital subjects, so the tabletop optics need to make the distinction. “POCKET UNIVERSE” is clear at 480.

Priorities: warp the actual stripe pattern through each lens, and use coloured rims and cast shadows to give the bodies weight and transparency.

### 153 · Lock Within · 1,569 bytes

Orange and cream frame a readable but small assembly that floats in too much bare space. The layered faces and patterned axes are a good economical fit, while cuboid pins and a keyhole icon make the diagram feel generic. The exploded lock is a distinct subject from earlier guides and technical plates. “LOCK WITHIN” and the smaller, thinner “FOUR PIN STUDY” are both readable at 480.

Priorities: enlarge the assembly and give the pins round cross-sections, and show the interior of the block so the axes terminate in visible chambers.

### 154 · Two on the Ice · 1,605 bytes

The criss-crossing curves generate movement, and the coat colours help pick out two otherwise tiny figures. At either size the lines resemble a drawn path diagram and the angular ice banks look arbitrary. Connected strokes give smooth track shapes cheaply, but the abrupt tails and heavy blue crossings hurt the illusion of blade marks. The overhead duet distinguishes it from earlier ball sports and ski material. “TWO ON THE ICE” is clear at 480.

Priorities: make the tracks read as pairs of shallow cuts and place larger skaters at opposite ends, oriented by the path tangent.

### 155 · Updraft of Umbrellas · 2,204 bytes

The tilted coral foreground umbrella reads well against the teal diagonal. Rotated polygons and native hook arcs suit this economical scene, but the repeat silhouette and ruler-straight storm bands freeze the motion. The umbrella flock is distinct from earlier weather graphics. “UPDRAFT OF” and “UMBRELLAS” are both readable at 480, but the last word on the first line hugs the angular title panel edge.

Priorities: turn one canopy inside out and vary the others' viewing angles, and replace the flat bands with curved gusts that carry rain into the composition.
