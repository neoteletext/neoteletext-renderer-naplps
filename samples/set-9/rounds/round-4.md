# Set 9 · Improvement round 4

## Critiques and priorities

I inspected the starting renders at 480 × 460 and 640 × 480. The narrow viewport makes the circular objects taller and makes some one-pixel marks harder to distinguish. Text assessments below are for 480 × 460.

### 146 · Paper Thermal

The two wing tips give the picture a strong upward spread, and cream and orange separate cleanly from violet. Flat NAPLPS polygons suit folded paper, although the three near-concentric floor shapes feel like a rug and the displaced dark shape feels pasted on. The folded bird is distinct from the earlier bird portraits, and treating it as a lit object is the more original part of the idea. “PAPER THERMAL” reads clearly. Give the light a direction by projecting a window onto the floor and carrying one shadow across its panes. Add cut edges and finer facet structure to the wings so they read as paper.

### 147 · Soap Film

The skewed square sets off the spectral fills, which are clean where the thin bright lines follow the contours. The four dark islands look like outlined pebbles, the lower purple quarter is underworked, and the taped-looking square frame makes the membrane feel rigid. “SOAP FILM” is clear. The draining membrane is a distinct optical study, though the broad colour stripes alone resemble a generic spectrum. Give the thin patches asymmetric advancing edges, and show pooled film feeding a curved meniscus and droplets.

### 148 · Final Spin

The pale green drum draws the eye and the pink sign is clear against the storefront. The frontal triptych suits NAPLPS circles and palette blink, but it still resembles a row of menu tiles. The street is almost black with scratchy horizontal reflections, and the active bay has little visible light beyond the drum. “FINAL SPIN” is easy to read. There have been streets and storefronts before, but one machine still spinning in a dark shop is a distinct story. Show the green light spilling across the centre bay and pavement, and render more of the rotating drum and laundry.

### 149 · Rabbit in the Wall

The dark rabbit and warm hand make a clear left-to-right reveal. Filled silhouettes are a good fit for shadow play, and the transformation from hand to rabbit makes this a different subject from an animal portrait. The spotlight still looks like concentric timber grain, especially at 640 × 480. The hand has angular fingertips and the eye is a perfect punched-out dot. “RABBIT IN THE WALL” reads clearly. Give the spotlight a much softer falloff and render the hand as a set of overlapping, rounded forms with a more natural eye glint in the shadow.

### 150 · Wind Organ

The rising arrangement and warm terracotta against chartreuse are immediately identifiable. The repeated polygons and bore ellipses work as a NAPLPS vocabulary, but each pipe still looks like a flat bar with a lid. The thin white air lines vanish against the pale sky in places, and the black shadows are more prominent than their mounts. “WIND” and “ORGAN” are both clear. The invented outdoor instrument has a strong premise beyond the earlier landscape studies. Model the pipe walls and hollow mouths, and connect the near-mouth flow into a more readable wind pattern.

### 151 · Kiln Opening

The arch frames the three silhouettes and the opening has a convincing hot-orange core against the dark masonry. WAIT and the palette change make the reveal integral to the scene. The hot bands are still almost perfectly symmetric, and all three pots have the same kind of neat stripes. “KILN OPENING” is readable with room to spare. The ceramics and their reveal give this a distinct subject among the set's mechanical and optical studies. Show an uneven heat pattern across the refractory bricks and distinguish the vessels through three different glaze treatments.

### 152 · Pocket Universe

Three well-spaced spheres and purple, cyan and orange give the refraction study a clear hierarchy. Clipped NAPLPS fills carry the cloth pattern into the balls, but the stripes end abruptly inside the rim, the nearly identical white panes look like logos, and the shadows are neat coloured targets. “POCKET UNIVERSE” is clear. The glass still life recalls the earlier glass studies, and the visible transmission of a woven pattern is its most individual feature. Carry the bands closer to the curved rim and give the three reflections and light on the cloth different forms.

### 153 · Lock Within

Orange and ivory make the floating components stand out, and the dotted guides and layered polygons suit the exploded view. The ends of the springs make dark hooks, and the small rectangular window leaves the plug looking like a horizontal bar. “LOCK WITHIN” and “FOUR PIN STUDY” are both readable. The cutaway gives this mechanical subject a distinct angle even beside earlier technical diagrams. Show the curved plug through a broader cutaway, and give the contrasting spring faces clear ends and depth.

### 154 · Two on the Ice

The diagonal spacing of the skaters leaves generous room for the interlaced cuts. The light ice and saturated clothing work together, and long NAPLPS paths make the strongest lines in the piece. Some thin parts almost vanish at 480 × 460, and the figures look like tangles of sticks, especially the dark skater. “TWO ON THE ICE” is clear. The overhead marks make it a different treatment from earlier sports pages. Give each figure a readable lean and distinct skates, and make the older cuts continuous enough to follow.

### 155 · Updraft of Umbrellas

The central orange umbrella has excellent contrast against the dark teal and the group rises convincingly from left to right. Reusing a polygon shape at different angles plays to NAPLPS, but the many parallel arcs look like a racetrack and the smaller umbrellas mostly share the same silhouette. “UPDRAFT OF” and “UMBRELLAS” both read. A flock of escaping umbrellas is a specific, playful departure from earlier weather pages. Exchange the repeated long bands for streams that break and curl around the canopies, and give the leading fabric and the exposed underside different structures.
