# Twenty-five independent services, 011–035

These are the 25 purposes and styles in `CONCEPTS.txt`, in the specified order. Each page has its own palette, composition and service identity. No collection masthead, shared footer or standard margin is embedded in the pages. The identifiers underneath thumbnails belong only to the review sheets. Names, services, prices, addresses, telephone numbers, programmes and news stories are fictional.

[640-render contact sheet](out/contact-sheet.png) · [Native 256 × 200 contact sheet](out/contact-sheet-256.png) · [Manifest](out/manifest.json) · [Validation results](out/validation.json) · [One-line summaries](out/summary.txt).

## Reproduce and inspect

Run `python3 render.py` to generate every stream and all four preview sizes, or `python3 render.py 011 026` to regenerate selected pages. Run `python3 validate.py` after the complete build. Each `pages/NNN-name.py` can also be executed directly from any working directory and writes its own `out/NNN-name.nap`. The encoder and generators use Python's standard library. The rendering/contact-sheet scripts additionally use Pillow, already available in this workspace, and the supplied release renderer and Microstar font. There are no downloaded artwork assets or external font substitutions.

Each stream has a 640 × 480 `.png`; `-256.png`, `-320.png` and `-512.png` are separate native renderer outputs at 256 × 200, 320 × 200 and 512 × 400. They are not scaled-down copies. The `review-NNN-NNN.png` sheets preserve five 640 × 480 previews at full size. Page 031 also has a second blink-phase PNG and a looping GIF illustrating the two phases. The `.nap` is the deliverable; the GIF is just a review copy.

## Encoding and checks

The streams begin `0E 20 7F 7F` (SO, full RESET), explicitly designate primary/PDI/supplementary/mosaic sets, set four-byte coordinates with DOMAIN, and define all 16 colour-map entries. The encoder uses the project's measured 1 × 25/32 viewport. Its pixel-based design coordinates are converted into that unit-screen space. Shapes remain native PDI rectangles, relative polygons, arcs and lines; only the downloadable glyph and the repeating pattern use the format's native masks. All ordinary lettering uses `microstar.strokes`. There are no input fields, cursor styles, word-wrap commands, transmit macros, or external bitmap pictures.

Every final page was visually reviewed at 640 × 480 and at native 256 × 200; revisions were re-rendered and reviewed. Important text uses character fields around 20 design pixels wide (1/32 screen) or larger. Compact dates, reference numbers and axis annotations use 17–19-pixel fields, still above the default 40-column field. No field is narrower than the default. The densest page deliberately remains the classifieds; its short lines preserve the small-ad character without using 80-column type.

Validation checks 25 deterministic generators, exact requested numbering, manifest byte counts, 7-bit data, initial full RESET, 16 explicit initial colour entries, the 6 KB ceiling, at most 16 rendered colours, all 100 output dimensions, and no renderer diagnostics. Each page was then rendered after a different predecessor at all four resolutions: all 100 RGB comparisons matched the standalone render. The maze is connected and has a unique 62-step start-to-finish solution; the bank balance and the market percentage also check out. Stream hashes and individual results are in `out/validation.json`. All 25 streams are below 3 KiB, ranging from 447 to 2,875 bytes; together they occupy 27,948 bytes.

Only 031 is animated. It uses no WAIT; a 15-tenth BLINK cycle changes four small corner bulbs, affecting 204 of 307,200 pixels. The typography, rules, other bulbs and complete listings remain constant. All 24 other pages contain neither WAIT nor BLINK, including 011's immediate palette redefinition. Every stream's final picture is complete.

## Renderer observations

**Entry zero in definition masks.** While defining the gingham pattern, `3E 40 1B 44 41` (select entry 0; DEF TEXTURE A) followed by filled rectangles produced an empty mask even though entry 0 was cream. Replacing the selection with `3E 44` (entry 1) made the same geometry populate the mask. `MaskSurface::put` in the existing renderer explicitly stores `entry != 0`. This is a possible mismatch with the guide's colour-independent description of masks, rather than a PP3-verified defect. Page 026 selects entry 1 before defining the pattern; validation reproduces the empty-versus-populated result. No renderer files were changed.

The documented limitations still apply: the CLI ignores WAIT for staging, and extreme display sizes can differ from PP3's stroke placement. There are no WAITs or rotated text in these pages; display type is concentrated in the catalogue, quiz and museum poster. No additional renderer faults or processing-limit warnings were observed.

## Individual designs

**011 — The Evening Ledger.** [Preview](out/011-evening-ledger.png). A newsprint front page with one large bridge-opening story and two short secondary headlines, separated by newspaper rules. A compact truss-bridge illustration anchors the lead column. The paper is drawn in entry 0 and then warmed from ivory to newsprint with one final SET COLOR: existing pixels recolour without redrawing or timed animation. Enlarged headlines and short, two-line reports keep the small version readable.

**012 — Belltown: five days.** [Preview](out/012-city-weather.png). Five tall, edge-separated pastel cards carry day, weather pictogram, high, low and a short condition. Native filled circles and rectangles make clouds and sun; thick diagonal rain strokes survive the smallest terminal. Large temperatures establish hierarchy while a lightly placed city/date header avoids the appearance of a dense dashboard.

**013 — Floodlight League.** [Preview](out/013-football.png). A six-club table uses condensed, fixed-pitch scoreboard lettering on alternating deep-green strips. Acid yellow marks the leader; pale text and aligned P/W/GD/PTS columns do the remaining work. Last night's two scores occupy a separate black-green strip, with four real G3 mosaic top-row glyphs serving as scoreboard lamps. No lamp blinks.

**014 — Meridian market close.** [Preview](out/014-market-close.png). Amber and black, widely tracked terminal text and a single continuous index trace evoke a financial display. The trace is native two-bit INCREMENTAL LINE data, including direction reversals, against dotted grid rules with an explicit logical pel. The final point aligns with the 1,284.6 headline; the two stock quotes and +3.36% close are fictional. The percentage is consistent with a 41.8-point gain.

**015 — North Quay Bank statement.** [Preview](out/015-bank-statement.png). Navy on cool grey, a small bank pediment and thin horizontal rules create a sober banking form. Account identity is separate from a three-line transaction register, whose fixed-width amounts align on the right. A solid navy closing-balance panel makes $2,560.00 the primary figure; opening $1,240 plus salary $2,100 less rent $780 reconciles exactly.

**016 — Homepost autumn offers.** [Preview](out/016-home-catalogue.png). A full-bleed red masthead and unequal-colour yellow/cyan/red merchandise panels recreate the confidence of a small 1980s catalogue. The pocket radio, jointed desk lamp and kettle are distinct native vector silhouettes with recognisable details. Three cream price bursts hold the largest product information. Order codes and postage remain secondary, with no repeated collection footer.

**017 — Coastlink intercity.** [Preview](out/017-intercity.png). A white arrow block and oversized station-style title sit on deep railway blue. Five rows align departure, destination, arrival and platform, using fixed-width times and stronger destination lettering. A cyan route line beneath the table links four named stops; its station discs are invocations of a relative ARC macro, so they share geometry without being absolute-position drawings.

**018 — Alderfield departures.** [Preview](out/018-departures.png). A yellow aircraft symbol and four rows of individual dark character cells suggest a settled split-flap board. Native colour mode 2 paints text fields; black dividers and a lower flap edge articulate the cells. An early seam through the letter centres was removed because it damaged low-resolution reading. TIME/FLT/TO/GT/STATE remain clearly separated, and no simulated interaction is implied.

**019 — A piece of the whole.** [Preview](out/019-fractions.png). Three chalk-coloured pies show one half, one third and three quarters on a slate-green board. Filled sector polygons and native circle outlines make the fraction areas literal. The bottom example equates 1/2 with 2/4 using a four-cell bar; its shaded half uses real cross-hatching with a defined pel. A slightly irregular perimeter line gives a hand-ruled board rather than a formal chart.

**020 — Round goes the rain.** [Preview](out/020-water-cycle.png). A friendly textbook landscape connects a sun, a cloud, falling rain, a snow-topped mountain, a stream and collecting water. Thick arrows distinguish the rising moisture from the cloud's movement. Four concise labels carry the lesson. The evaporation arrow was moved rightward after review so it no longer crossed its own word.

**021 — Les petits mots.** [Preview](out/021-french-cards.png). Four slightly offset cream flash cards pair small coloured pictures with école/school, forêt/forest, café/coffee and été/summer. Accents are actual supplementary-set non-spacing sequences: acute `19 42` and circumflex `19 43` precede the corresponding primary letter. The school, forest, steaming cup and sun keep the page playful without shrinking the words into vocabulary lists.

**022 — Your turn.** [Preview](out/022-noughts-crosses.png). Red and blue marker strokes sit on pale ruled notebook paper with a pink margin. The grid uses incremental lines with minute deviations instead of mechanical rectangles; X marks use broad, slightly bent strokes, and O marks use native outlined circles. Three Xs and three Os make the empty centre, middle-right and lower-left legal options for X's seventh move. The lower grid strokes were shortened to clear the turn caption.

**023 — The long way out.** [Preview](out/023-maze.png). A bold coral labyrinth occupies almost the entire yellow page, with a broad title and clear entry/exit arrows. A seeded depth-first generator produces a 14 × 8 perfect maze, encoded as thick native walls. The entrance is upper left and exit lower right; passage widths and wall thickness remain distinct at 256 × 200. Validation establishes connectivity, uniqueness and the 62-step solution without putting the answer in the picture.

**024 — Bright Spark.** [Preview](out/024-quiz-show.png). Two theatrical spotlights and six fixed lamps lead into a large two-line planet question. Four chamfered blue and magenta answer panels, each with a dark circular letter badge, give the game-show structure. All four choices remain equally plausible visually; Saturn is the answer, but no selected state or interactive input field is encoded. Nothing blinks.

**025 — Recovery position.** [Preview](out/025-recovery-position.png). Red and white public-safety typography frames a large side-lying adult pictogram, with the bent upper leg picked out in red. Four short steps cover supporting the cheek, bending the far knee, rolling, opening the airway and monitoring. The eligibility line specifies unresponsiveness, normal breathing and no suspected injury; Australia’s 000 is used. The copy follows the principles in [NHS inform's recovery-position guidance](https://www.nhsinform.scot/tests-and-treatments/first-aid/how-to-put-someone-in-the-recovery-position/) and the [Resuscitation Council UK's first-aid guidance](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/first-aid-guidelines); the final strip addresses abnormal breathing. It is presented as a fictional period public-information page.

**026 — Sunday pancakes.** [Preview](out/026-pancakes.png). A warm recipe card combines four large ingredient lines, a short two-line method and a layered stack of pancakes with syrup and butter. The red gingham perimeter uses a genuine DEF TEXTURE A checker mask tiled at a 24-pixel design size; the centre is overprinted with cream. The pattern was initially invisible because the renderer erased its zero-index mask geometry; the nonzero selection workaround restores the intended border.

**027 — 18 Fern Close.** [Preview](out/027-fern-close.png). A cream-and-olive estate brochure leads with the street and a dark address-number block. The left illustration builds the house from two wall faces, a gable and two pitched roof planes, with windows and doorway respecting the same projection. A right-hand information stack gives price, bedrooms, bathroom and 126-square-metre floor area above a small plan. The agency appears as a dedicated local contact band.

**028 — Small ads.** [Preview](out/028-classifieds.png). Six ruled plum-on-white boxes present sale, accommodation, lessons, lost property, market and repair notices. The compact title/body hierarchy and short telephone-led copy preserve density while avoiding microtype. Only the central market advertisement has a yellow ground, making a believable paid display upgrade. It shares neither the newspaper masthead nor that page's paper colour or column rhythm.

**029 — Libra: the balanced day.** [Preview](out/029-libra.png). A large gold zodiac wheel with twelve radial compartments and repeated star macros balances a narrow horoscope column on deep purple. The central scales are an actual defined DRCS character, drawn at a larger character size after the stars are complete; G3 is explicitly redesignated from macro to DRCS for that invocation. Old gold, lavender and cream give mystical hierarchy without animation or illegible ornamental type.

**030 — Tonight on three.** [Preview](out/030-tv-tonight.png). Three colour-coded channel tiles key a light electronic schedule grid. One-hour blocks coexist with two-hour merged film and cricket listings; the time axis is fixed pitch and the programmes are concise. Vertical construction lines originally crossed the merged text; final time ticks stop at the grid's top, leaving those listings unobstructed. The schedule covers 18:00 to 21:00.

**031 — The Aster cinema.** [Preview](out/031-aster-cinema.png) · [Blink preview](out/031-aster-cinema.gif). Wine-red ground, restrained gold outlines and repeated native-arc bulb macros surround three centred film listings. The listing titles, times, ratings and box-office number remain static. Only four corner bulbs use colour entry 8's BLINK to entry 2: `3F 48 45 4A 40`, five tenths amber and ten tenths cream, no delay. The final still is fully legible in either phase; validation checks both phases and the return after 15 tenths.

**032 — Form in motion.** [Preview](out/032-form-in-motion.png). A deliberately asymmetric Bauhaus museum poster uses a red square with a large native M, an unadorned blue circle, a small black square and a cropped yellow triangle. The exhibition title anchors the left, dates and venue occupy the top, and the lower-left copy supplies subject and opening hours. Display type is intentional here; the event information stays at a conventional reading scale. At 447 bytes it is the smallest stream.

**033 — Old Quay city centre.** [Preview](out/033-old-quay-map.png). Wide pale streets divide muted city blocks, a green park, a civic square and three numbered landmarks. A blue river defines the map's eastern edge. The separate unframed key explains museum, hall, rail and the coral “you are here” star; a north arrow and a 200-metre bar supply cartographic orientation. Street labels sit inside their corridors instead of obscuring buildings.

**034 — Alder Metro.** [Preview](out/034-alder-metro.png). Three red, blue and green routes use only horizontal, vertical and 45-degree segments. Small rings identify ordinary stations; larger black-and-cream rings mark all three route crossings as interchanges. Short station names are placed clear of the coloured strokes, and a compact three-swatch route key occupies the lower-right space. Its schematic geometry and light ground distinguish it from both the street map and railway timetable.

**035 — One-transistor amplifier.** [Preview](out/035-amplifier.png). Thin white conductors and component symbols sit over a subdued blue construction grid. A plausible common-emitter NPN circuit has a 100k/22k bias divider, 4k7 collector resistor, 1k emitter resistor, 1uF input capacitor and 10uF collector output capacitor, powered from +9V. Filled junction dots, an outward emitter arrow and a shared ground make the connections explicit. Cyan IN/OUT/Q1 labels provide hierarchy; component values remain large enough for the small terminal.
