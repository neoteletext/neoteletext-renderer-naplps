# Set 6 · Round 1

I inspected the original stills at 480 × 460 and 640 × 480. The original images are preserved in [round-0](round-0/).

## 121 · Afterimage

The inward sweep is strong and the two lime blades immediately catch the eye. The iris fills the square-ish 480 × 460 viewport well as an oval and reads as a circular instrument at 640 × 480, where the side space feels particularly empty. The almost-black blade gaps are doing more to establish depth than the violet faces, which are flat and too similar. Several mint highlights look like raster scratches. Both AFTERIMAGE and PHASE / 06 are legible at 480 × 460. The pulse tied to the strip is a strong use of native palette animation and fits the theme, though a mechanical iris is a familiar motif and the housing barely distinguishes this one.

Priorities

1. Machine the housing with clear inset bands, major graduations and registration marks.
2. Give the blades bevels and channel details while keeping the moving colour and sharpening the hub.

## 122 · The Lure

The high lure and low body form a clear diagonal. The dive beam frames the fish but its flat capped bands read as a cutout, especially at 640 × 480. Blue-black water and acid green are a good contrast pair. The oversized mauve lip, bright identical teeth and tiny round eye make the reveal feel like a cartoon grin. Neither the angular tail nor the solid dorsal triangles explain much about the animal. THE LURE and BATHYPELAGIC / 1400 M are fully legible at 480 × 460. WAIT and palette redefinition make the image genuinely native to the medium, but the fish silhouette and glow symbol are generic.

Priorities

1. Build a darker, more articulated fish with ribbed fins, an embedded eye and teeth rooted in the jaw.
2. Use the timing and palette change to reveal uneven reflected light, suspended particles and a graded lure.

## 123 · Fault Line

The broad diagonal and strong bone, carbon and oxide contrast do most of the work. FAULT parses at 480 × 460, with the nearly touching U and L requiring a second look. LETTERPRESS IN DISPLACEMENT, FAULT LINE and INK / PAPER / SHEAR all read clearly. The 640 × 480 still exposes the poster's large untouched colour fields and the footer brackets look like a placeholder device. Polygon splitting is an inventive fit for NAPLPS and the theme. The displaced headline is an original treatment within a familiar split-type poster composition. The finish does little to suggest actual ink or paper.

Priorities

1. Add offset impression edges and a stratified tear, keeping the letter fragments readable.
2. Work restrained paper and ink texture into the fields and replace the footer brackets with press registration details.

## 124 · Breathing Earth

The mound is well centred and its pale sand, clay and dark chambers read at both sizes. BREATHING EARTH, TERMITE MOUND / SECTION, AIR SHAFT, GARDEN and BROOD CHAMBER are legible at 480 × 460. The air shaft leader terminates at a chamber and the garden is another black oval. The labels promise more differentiation than the drawing delivers. The thick uniform connectors end abruptly, and the quiet soil looks like two flat bands under a floating cutaway. The custom stipple is a good use of NAPLPS and supports the theme. A termite mound is an original subject for the set, but the simple node-and-line interior undersells it.

Priorities

1. Carve layered passages with differentiated garden and brood contents, and show air movement in the shaft.
2. Seat the cutaway in layered, textured ground and point each leader at the feature it actually names.

## 125 · Selvedge Moth

The symmetry and warm forewing spots against cool hindwings give it a strong silhouette at both sizes. SELVEDGE MOTH is legible at 480 × 460. At 640 × 480 the empty cloth and abrupt light body stand out more, and gaps between tiles read as seams in some places and as broken joins in others. The eyespots are coarse stepped blocks and the border is mostly an outline. Reflecting custom DRCS half-tiles is the clearest medium-specific concept in the set. The woven moth is an original fit for the theme, but the present piece looks more like a pixel emblem than textile.

Priorities

1. Add programmed stitch detail within the wings and a segmented body that belongs to the same fabric.
2. Make the border and ground look woven with repeat motifs, warp threads and visible selvedges.

## Changes made

- **Afterimage** gained a concentric machined housing, a graduated rim and side registration marks. The blades now have bevels, channels and vent marks, and the hub has a layered bearing. The six-phase sweep still drives the paired blades and the caption strip.
- **The Lure** gained a rebuilt silhouette with fin ribs, a recessed eye and irregular teeth seated in the jaw. Three layers of water and more varied particles establish the beam. The last stage lights the curved filament, stepped halo and reflected ridge together.
- **Fault Line** gained overprinted impression edges, grain in both paper and letter faces, and a multi-layer tear. The two upper U strokes are clipped independently to prevent a bridge across the opening. Ink swatches and a registration target replace the brackets.
- **Breathing Earth** gained layered passages, airflow chevrons, a green garden and pale brood shapes. Sediment bands, roots and mineral flecks anchor the mound. The air-shaft leader lands on the passage and the other leaders meet the filled chambers.
- **Selvedge Moth** gained two mirrored DRCS stitch masks, a segmented body, a thread grid, diamond border repeats and stitched side edges. The original tile silhouette remains visible through the added surface detail.

Removed the stream-size ceiling from the validator and the encoder's save method. Rebuilt the streams, both preview sizes, contact sheet and manifest. Reviewed the final renders at both sizes. All artwork text reads at 480 × 460, and validation passed for all five streams, including the six BLINK phases and three WAIT stages.
