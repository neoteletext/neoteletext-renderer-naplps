# Improvement round 2

## Before the changes

Inspected all ten current previews at their native sizes. The narrower terminal makes the iris noticeably taller and makes the small marks on all five pages less forgiving.

### 121 · Afterimage

The central spiral grabs the eye and the opposed lime blades make the rotation easy to imagine. The inactive faces collapse into a similar violet, while the black slits are so heavy that the iris resembles a turbine. The ticks almost touch the top edge. The side brackets hang in blank space and the face machining is reduced to disconnected dashes. AFTERIMAGE and PHASE / 06 are both legible at 480 × 460. Native circles and shared BLINK entries make this a convincing picture for the medium. A rotating iris is a familiar motif, but pairing opposite blades with a linked index gives this one an identity.

- Fit the full assembly inside deliberate margins and give the housing a readable hierarchy of scales.
- Layer the blade surfaces and place trailing highlights behind the live pair so the timing leaves a visible afterimage.

### 122 · The Lure

The fish sits at the end of a strong left-to-right beam and the lure holds the right third. The bright back reads as a broad flat stripe, the mouth as a straight-edged grin, and the flat beam overwhelms the shape of the water. The tail and fins are particularly stiff at 480 × 460. THE LURE and BATHYPELAGIC / 1400 M are both readable there. The anglerfish silhouette is familiar, but the WAIT reveal and indexed illumination make a genuinely medium-specific encounter.

- Build a layered beam with silt of varying sizes and quieter streaks.
- Model the jaw, gill, fins and lure with graduated local light so the reveal describes the animal's volume.

### 123 · Fault Line

Bone, oxide and carbon give the poster a strong two-field composition. LETTERPRESS IN DISPLACEMENT, FAULT LINE and INK / PAPER / SHEAR all read at 480 × 460. The huge FAULT is decipherable there, although the bottom of the F looks like stray debris and the narrow black A counter is doing a lot of work. At both sizes the rupture looks like a smooth stripe and the rectangular flecks resemble digital noise. Clipping and overpainting make good use of flat indexed polygons. A split headline is a familiar poster device. The physical handling of the paper and ink can make this version distinctive.

- Make the break jagged and fibrous while keeping both word fragments readable.
- Give the letter edges convincing misregistration and shape the A counter to fit the displaced upper face.

### 124 · Breathing Earth

The ochre mound is the clear centre and the label leaders make the section easy to scan. BREATHING EARTH, TERMITE MOUND / SECTION, AIR SHAFT, GARDEN and BROOD CHAMBER all read at 480 × 460. The same ovals repeat as chambers and the stiff channels look like disconnected pipes. Tiny airflow marks vanish into them at the smaller size. The repeatable NAPLPS stipple suits compact earth, and the explanatory cutaway adds welcome variety. The drawing is still close to a generic diagram because it shows little evidence of what built or inhabits the mound.

- Turn the pipe network into a continuous cavity with distinct chamber shapes and a clear route for airflow.
- Show individual termites and develop the garden and brood as different habitable spaces.

### 125 · Selvedge Moth

The symmetry and warm eyespots make the moth readable, with blue-green lower wings holding their own at both sizes. SELVEDGE MOTH is clear at 480 × 460, though the lowest wing tiles crowd its rule. The graph-paper background, repeated diagonal stamp and loose-looking borders make the piece feel assembled from tiles. Reflected DRCS masks are a good fit for woven forms. The symmetrical pixel moth is familiar, but distinct stitch structures and actual selvedges could make the treatment its own.

- Give the eyespots, wing bands and half-tiles different stitch structures.
- Turn the uniform grid and thin border into warp, weft and bound edges, with breathing room above the title.

## Changes made

- **Afterimage** moved the iris inward to clear the viewport edges and caption. Added raised scale markers, fasteners, side housings and layered blade bevels. Each phase also lights a thin channel one blade behind each active face, so the pulse leaves a two-part wake without adding a timing phase.
- **The Lure** built up the dive beam with suspended silt and oblique streaks. The last reveal now lights two planes across the back, fin rays, gill folds, the jaw rim and the belly contour. Expanded the lure halo with closer colour steps and a concentrated core.
- **Fault Line** replaced the smooth seam with serrated paper layers and exposed fibres. Offset two impressions along the broken letter edges, reduced the lower fragment's displacement so the foot of the F tracks its stem, and opened a larger counter in the A with flecks that carry the paper grain through it.
- **Breathing Earth** drew continuous dark passages with irregular chambers, strengthened the airflow chevrons and added six termites. Green ridged clusters and highlighted brood shapes distinguish the two occupied chambers.
- **Selvedge Moth** made reflected DRCS masks for running stitches, satin stitches, cross-shaped eyespot knots and stitched diagonal edges. Reworked the ground as staggered warp and weft, bound both selvedges and raised the wing tips above the title rule.

Regenerated the five streams, ten PNG previews, contact sheet and manifest. Inspected both updated sizes and read every label at 480 × 460. Validation passed for deterministic generation, manifest and preview agreement, full reset, all 16 colour-map entries, 7-bit data, clean renderer diagnostics, six distinct BLINK phases and three distinct WAIT stages. The sources already had no byte-size ceiling. Removed the validator's per-page size output and kept the descriptive byte counts in the manifest.
