# Set 6 · Improvement round 3

## Before the changes

I reviewed each current render at 480 × 460 and 640 × 480. The narrower screen makes the circular elements noticeably taller and compresses the horizontal lettering and fine detail.

### Afterimage

The centred iris has a strong silhouette and the opposite lime blades make the sweep immediately visible against the violet. The cyan title and dial marks read cleanly, but the navy-violet casing is nearly featureless around the sides. At 640 × 480 the blade seams and concentric rings look like an instrument. At 480 × 460 the tiny attachment plates look stranded and the dark notches are easier to see than the shallow relief. “AFTERIMAGE” and “PHASE / 06” are both legible. Palette-driven motion is one of the set's most convincing uses of NAPLPS. The iris is a familiar shape, with originality in the paired pulse and its instrument treatment.

Priorities

- Make the outer housing read as a driven mechanism, with teeth and attachments that connect to the ring.
- Give each vane a second tier of machining and a physical seam that survives the pulse.

### The Lure

The silhouette entering a flashlight beam gives the WAIT stages a reason to exist, and the cyan dorsal edge, chartreuse teeth and small eye carry the final reveal. The beam itself looks like several broad ribbons with hard elbows, most obvious at 640 × 480. The lure arc stops just outside the round halo and looks detached at both sizes. The fish's dark underside is readable as a broad shape but feels empty next to the busy teeth. “THE LURE” and “BATHYPELAGIC / 1400 M” both read at 480 × 460. A toothy anglerfish is familiar imagery, but the staged searchlight gives it a page-specific point of view and fits the theme.

Priorities

- Replace the ribbon-like beam with a graded cone with suspended particles that follow its light.
- Physically join the stalk and luminous organ, and give the halo softer transitions.
- Develop the lower head and tail with restrained, reflected detail.

### Fault Line

The diagonal is a good compositional engine and black, bone and oxide make the displaced letters and print marks easy to distinguish. The oversized “FAULT” can be reconstructed at 480 × 460 and “LETTERPRESS IN DISPLACEMENT”, “FAULT LINE” and “INK / PAPER / SHEAR” are all legible. The evenly scattered square flecks look like digital noise, especially across the empty top, and the single busy white edge looks like a zipper at 640 × 480. Fractured display typography is familiar, but treating the split as a printing event is a distinctive fit for a medium with editable polygonal type and overpainting.

Priorities

- Trade the even speckle for paper fibres and clusters of ink dropout.
- Make the cut visibly shear and branch, with broken fibres concentrated in plausible areas.
- Make the registration mark and ink samples look like parts of the same press proof.

### Breathing Earth

The pale field and ochre cutaway are easy to scan, and the three leaders leave room for the section to tell the story. “BREATHING EARTH”, “TERMITE MOUND / SECTION”, “AIR SHAFT”, “GARDEN” and “BROOD CHAMBER” all read at 480 × 460. The chambers look like black beads on rigid pipes, the mound surface resembles flat orange card, and the exposed ground is horizontally banded with identical flecks. At 640 × 480 some of the termite detail becomes visible, but at 480 × 460 it mostly reads as flecks. It's a less predictable subject than the other pages, and the custom fill and connected paths suit a field-guide plate. The diagram currently shows one exhaust path and little to suggest air entering.

Priorities

- Give the cut face an irregular clay structure and make the soil layers visually distinct.
- Show a second airflow path feeding the central chimney, with repeated directional marks.
- Improve the definition of the garden contents and differentiating details at ground level.

### Selvedge Moth

The frame and hand-worked edges establish the textile idea. Clay and pale gold give the forewing markings good contrast against indigo, while the quieter teal spots balance the lower half. “SELVEDGE MOTH” reads at both sizes. At 640 × 480 the programmable stitch motifs are visible, but at 480 × 460 the broad grey cells dominate and the wings look like mirrored stacks of teeth. The separate body sits on top of that grid like a zipper. This is the strongest match between subject and NAPLPS machinery in the set, using glyphs as mirrored embroidery. Symmetrical moth motifs are familiar, so the stitch logic and the shaping need to do the original work.

Priorities

- Re-chart the wing edges and separate the forewing and hindwing with an intentional fold.
- Add directional stitch glyphs for veins and use a contrasting band to articulate the eyespots.
- Give the thorax and abdomen a layered stitch treatment that meets the wings.

## Changes made

- **Afterimage** added a 48-tooth ring gear, connected mounting brackets with slotted bolts, and machined channels and fasteners on the vanes. The static relief remains visible through the travelling pulse.
- **The Lure** rebuilt the dive light as a graduated cone with suspended silt, extended the stalk into the luminous organ, and added reflected light to the lower jaw and tail webbing. The empty beam, silhouette and illuminated fish retain their three stages.
- **Fault Line** added offsets and branching tears to the fracture, changed the paper texture to directional fibres, clustered the ink dropout and connected the ink swatches to an offset registration target. Snapped the tear vertices to the coordinate grid to keep the edge flush with the viewport.
- **Breathing Earth** opened a ground-level intake and showed airflow turning into the chimney. Added clay facets and seams, grass at the surface, distinct textures through the soil and brighter ridges in the garden chamber.
- **Selvedge Moth** redrew the wing chart with tapered half-tiles and a fold between the forewing and hindwing. Added reflected DRCS stitch patterns for diagonal veins and eyespot bands, and extended the layered thorax stitching into the wings.

Regenerated the five streams, ten native-size previews, contact sheet and manifest. Saved the previews and contact sheet in the round archive. Reviewed both sizes and read every label at 480 × 460. The smallest termite figures still read as tiny silhouettes at that size. Validation passed for deterministic streams, manifest and preview agreement, full reset, all 16 colour-map entries, 7-bit data, clean renderer diagnostics, six BLINK phases and three WAIT stages. The validator and generators already had no stream-size ceiling. The manifest byte counts are descriptive.
