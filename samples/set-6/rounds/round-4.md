# Round 4

## Critique and priorities

I reviewed the incoming PNGs at their native 480 × 460 and 640 × 480 sizes.

### 121 · Afterimage

The iris fills the frame decisively, and the side mounts keep the tall 480 × 460 oval from looking stranded. Teal and violet give the dark hardware a credible material separation, but the near-solid yellow blades dominate so completely that the hairline wake gets lost. The ragged bright shapes look more like chipped paint than light traveling over machined surfaces. At 640 × 480 the circular instrument is more convincing, but the little central lozenge still looks like a placeholder. Native circles, repeating polygons and indexed animation make it a strong medium-specific picture. The turbine silhouette is familiar, while the opposing pulses and linked phase display give it some identity. At 480 × 460, “AFTERIMAGE” and “PHASE / 06” both read cleanly.

Priorities: Shape the pulse into a machined inset with a legible wake. Turn the flat center into an optical element. Give the mounts and phase display a stronger physical connection to the instrument.

### 122 · The Lure

The fish occupies the center well and its glowing bait pulls the eye up and right. The background beam looks like stacked curved paper with a blunt end, and its broad midtone bands compete with the dark fish. The unmodulated dorsal spikes and evenly cut triangles in the mouth make the fish look like a cartoon prop. The fins and jaw are wider at 640 × 480 but the basic flatness persists. WAIT staging and a palette change suit a scene built around a delayed encounter. A monster with a light is familiar imagery, and the lure would feel more distinctive if it were catching something. “THE LURE” and “BATHYPELAGIC / 1400 M” are both legible at 480 × 460, including the slashed zeros.

Priorities: Make the dive beam narrow and recede. Put anatomically directed relief into the head and jaw and vary the teeth. Give the lure an object of attention in the last reveal.

### 123 · Fault Line

Carbon above oxide and pale reversed ink below make the main diagonal read immediately in either size. The displacement registers, but the narrow, uniformly frayed trim makes the two faces look glued together. The A counter is readable; the upper T is barely attached to its stem, and the U only resolves when the eye joins its widely displaced pieces. At 640 × 480 the pinstripe shadows and pointy loose strands are more apparent. Clipped polygons and overprinting make an effective vector-native letterpress illusion. The cut-word poster is a familiar device; the inverse printing across the torn stock is the more original choice. “LETTERPRESS IN DISPLACEMENT,” “FAULT LINE,” and “INK / PAPER / SHEAR” all read at 480 × 460. The big “FAULT” takes a second pass despite the larger lettering.

Priorities: Give the diagonal a shadow, intermittent folds, and distinct stock layers. Register the upper and lower letter fragments more closely and integrate them into the physical tear.

### 124 · Breathing Earth

The dark internal network reads against warm clay, the cream callout works over the soil, and the three leaders make the cutaway understandable. The mound looks squat at 640 × 480 but balanced at 480 × 460. The bright dashes in the cavities barely register as insects even at the larger size, and the right shaft looks plugged at the top. The underground terrain has texture, but the isolated broad bands make it feel diagrammatically flat. Native patterned fill and overlaid routes make good use of the medium; the field-guide treatment fits the varied set, although the diagram is conventional. “BREATHING EARTH,” “TERMITE MOUND / SECTION,” “AIR SHAFT,” “GARDEN,” and “BROOD CHAMBER” all read at 480 × 460.

Priorities: Show a termite in a magnified detail connected to its chamber. Make the openings and airflow continuous. Give the surrounding sediment a sense of bedding.

### 125 · Selvedge Moth

The symmetry is assertive and the rust wings pop against indigo. The cool lower spots are quieter and the dull blue in between makes the outer contours feel blocky. The loomed ground reads at both sizes, but the large bright spots look like adjacent buttons and the angular body looks like an applique badge. The side stitches stop abruptly at the lower border. “SELVEDGE MOTH,” the only text, is legible in the footer at 480 × 460. Reflected DRCS masks are the most specific use of the medium in the set, and the textile construction is more distinctive than the familiar symmetric moth motif.

Priorities: Replace the paired square highlights with centered embroidered eyespots and carry veins out from the body. Make the body look threaded and finish the cloth edges with knots and exposed warp.

## Changes made

- **121 · Afterimage** — Moved the opposing BLINK pulses from whole blade faces into recessed, ribbed lamp channels and kept the finer wake. Built a concentric optical hub with offset reflections, wired the mounting plates, and set the phase lights into individual bezels.
- **122 · The Lure** — Narrowed and raised the dive beam as it recedes, added curved gill plates and jaw muscles, and shaped the teeth with bends and lit edges. Three baitfish approach the lamp in the final WAIT reveal.
- **123 · Fault Line** — Built up the tear with variable-depth paper layers, cast shadows beneath its folds, and concentrated fibers around the buckled areas. Brought the two print passes closer into register, bent the letter fragments near the tear, and moved the A counter with its face.
- **124 · Breathing Earth** — Added an enlarged worker with antennae, six legs and a segmented abdomen, linked it to the chamber, extended the exhaust paths through both chimneys, and drew sloping sediment beds on either side.
- **125 · Selvedge Moth** — Stitched four centered eyespots and vein trails across the reflected DRCS tiles, added thread marks to the body, and finished the upper and lower hems with knotted warp fringe.
