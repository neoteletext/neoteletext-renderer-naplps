# Round 2: critique of the round-1 renders

The critique below is of the renders in `rounds/round-1/`, looked at in both sizes, 640 by 480 and 480 by 460. Round 1 fixed the faults named in `round-1.md`; what remains, and what the round-1 pages still lack, is set out here.

## 177 Good Morning, Hartley Avenue

Round 1 cured the empty sky and the orphaned badge. The page now reads as a morning front page. What is still missing: the menu rows are text alone, so the panel gives no sense of four different services; the street has one bus and nothing else; the house has no door light or porch, so the morning feeling depends on the sun alone. The four menu items would be read faster with a small mark beside each.

Changes planned: a mark beside each menu label (a sun for weather, an envelope for mail, a lunch box for school, a bus for the bus), with the labels moved right to make room; a row of lamp posts along the pavement's far edge; a thin rule between the menu rows.

## 178 A Card for Grandma

The tulips and the arc now read correctly, and the rosette frame gives the card a border. The greeting still looks like a typewriter: its strokes are the stroke font's default single pen, so the letters are thin against the cream. A card of this kind would have heavier lettering. The sparkles are small diamonds and feel like afterthoughts.

Changes planned: draw the greeting and the name with a heavier pen (three units, not one), so the strokes stand out; replace the four diamonds with four-armed sparkles drawn as crossed lines, larger; add a small heart above the bouquet, drawn as two discs and a triangle.

## 179 The Family Message Board

The board is the most finished page in the set. The pins are clear of the words, the notes are legible at 480 by 460, and the blink on JEN's pin is a visible change when the pin is in its other state. The page still has one bare patch, at the left between DAD and KEVIN, and the notes are all rectangles; nothing on the board is handwritten or drawn.

Changes planned: a red heart doodle on the cork beside JEN's note; a strip of masking tape across the top corner of DAD's note; a small shopping list pinned at the left edge, with ticked lines drawn as short strokes.

## 180 Supper Tonight

A clear recipe page. The title is above the steam, the dish has a bowl's shape, and the timer's eight green segments read as cooking. What the page lacks is the information a recipe card gives first: how long it takes and how many it feeds. The space to the right of the title is empty. The timer's "MIN LEFT" is set under the ring, which works, but the number 25 in the hub is the only figure the timer shows.

Changes planned: a line under the title with "SERVES 4 - COOK 25 MIN" in the space to its right; a small tick mark at each of the 12 timer segments' outer edges, for the clock feel; a small bowl icon in the ingredients heading.

## 181 The Wired Living Room

The scene reads now: the family is clear of the television, the menu is whole, the cat is a cat, and the picture hangs on the wall. What it still lacks is warmth and light. Every surface is lit the same way, the window is a flat blue, and nothing in the room is on.

Changes planned: a floor lamp beside the window with a warm shade drawn in the yellow entry; a striped rug in place of the plain disc; a small plant in a pot on the far right of the floor.

## 182 Neighbourhood Calendar, Maple Ridge Estates

The calendar is correct and legible, and the coming-up panel and the weekend tint make the page read as a month. It has no community detail on the grid itself, so it still reads as a generic month rather than this estate's. Events are marked only by colour and a word.

Changes planned: a small balloon cluster above the PARTY block on the 21st; a grey bin with a lid on each Thursday (5, 12, 19, 26), drawn as a rectangle and a flat lid, so the calendar carries the estate's bin day; a small garage-sale sign on the 27th drawn as a board on two posts.

## 183 The House That Orders Its Own Paper Towels

The strongest page for the medium: the cutaway, the gabled roof, the van and the flag all work, and the order form reads at 480 by 460. Its lack is the one the brief names: the house is a flat cutaway with nothing outside it, and the terminal has no sign that it is talking to the house or to the shop. The sky is clear.

Changes planned: a sun in the upper-left sky with short rays drawn as lines; radiating arcs beside the terminal to show it sending the order; a window with a blind in the house's right wall.

## 184 Home Watch

The floor plan now reads as a plan, with doors, windows, the dial and the porch lamp. The porch lamp in its off state is a dark ring with no light, which reads as a lamp switched off. The kitchen and the study are plain: the kitchen has its light only as a fill colour, and the study is empty.

Changes planned: a dashed line from the kitchen door to the hall marking an open path; a row of small indicator lights along the top of the status panel, one per room; a lamp drawn in the study as a small disc with a glow ring.

## 185 Tonight's Horoscope

The wheel, the planets, the moon, the reading and the lucky colours all work. The panel ends after the lucky colours, so the lower right of the picture is dark and unused, and the reading has no line for the day after.

Changes planned: a "TOMORROW" line under the lucky colours with a short reading; a row of five star marks with three filled, as a day's rating; the planets are unchanged.

## 186 School Bus Is Late

The bulletin reads at a glance: the headline, the road, the bus with its windscreen and door, and the snowflakes all work. The snowbank still covers the bus's wheels, so the bus looks like it is sinking into the snow, which is wrong for a bus that has stopped. The page has no detail that says the bus is the school's.

Changes planned: lower the snowbank so the wheels show above it; a red stop arm folded out from the bus's side, drawn as an octagon and a white bar; a line in the foot band giving a new arrival time, "NEW ARRIVAL 8:40 AM".

## Summary of the round

Of the set, 179 and 185 are the most finished. The pages that still need the most work are 177, 178 and 181, which are correct but lack detail and light; the changes above target those. Shared across the set: the stroke font's single pen makes the card's greeting thin, and the picture's light is flat in every page. The round-2 changes give the greeting a heavier pen and add light sources to 177, 181 and 184.
