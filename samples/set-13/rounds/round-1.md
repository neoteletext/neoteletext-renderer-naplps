# Round 1: critique of the round-0 renders

Each page was looked at in both sizes, 640 by 480 and 480 by 460, as the channel shows it. The critique below is of the renders in `rounds/round-0/`. Changes made in this round follow the critique, appended after it.

## 177 Good Morning, Hartley Avenue

Composition works: the sunrise is revealed as banded discs over a flat grass ground, the house stands in front of it, and the paper menu panel sits on the right under a teal header. The palette is warm and bold, which suits the 1983 poster idea. Weaknesses: the upper sky is an empty pale band with nothing in it, so the top half of the page is inert; the "2 NEW" badge floats between the MAIL label and the next row rather than belonging to MAIL; the menu's detail lines (11 by 16 units) are thin at 480 by 460; the panel's bottom edge crowds the road. The page has one animation idea (the sun) but the bus, which the menu names, never appears on the street.

Changes planned: move the badge onto the MAIL row; enlarge the detail lines to 12 by 18; fill the sky with a cloud of discs and a pair of birds; put a bus on the road for the THE BUS entry, revealed last.

## 178 A Card for Grandma

This is the weakest page. The tulips read as crowns, because the bowl has three flat tips and no body, and the bouquet is small against the card. The greeting follows an arc, which is the right idea, but the letters are spaced by angle, so the "H" and the comma sit far from the rest, and the stroke font at 24 by 34 looks thin. The card's top band and its lower corners are empty. The price line is correct and legible but small. The "rotated italic" the concept names is not available: NAPLPS rotates text in quarter turns, so the arc of single letters stands in for it, and the page should say so in DESIGN.md.

Changes planned: redraw each tulip with a rounded cup, a pale highlight facet and a stem with two crossed leaves; enlarge the bouquet by about half; set the greeting letters by arc length with larger type; add a rosette border and a bow at the ribbon; enlarge the price line.

## 179 The Family Message Board

The strongest composition so far: five notes at differing tilts, pins, drop shadows and a dot-patterned cork ground read at once as a household board. Weaknesses: the pin of the DOUG note sits on the words "I CAN", so pin and text collide; the note text starts close under the pin on every note; the body lines (12 by 18) are legible but are the smallest text on the page at 480 by 460; the left middle of the board is bare cork; the blink on the JEN pin shows only as red in the still.

Changes planned: move each note's text down and keep the pins clear of it; enlarge the body lines to 13 by 19; add a polaroid photograph of the family on a string at the left, drawn with a sky and a hill, so that the empty cork gains a use.

## 180 Supper Tonight

The recipe is clear and the layout is sound: a cutaway dish, ingredients on the left, the method on the right, the timer above the method. Faults: the grey steam lines run into the title "CHEDDAR NOODLE BAKE"; the dish is a box with straight sides, which reads as a tray rather than a casserole; the timer's blink turns eight of the twelve segments red in the still, so the state looks like "done" when the recipe is still cooking; "MIN LEFT" under the ring is small.

Changes planned: move the title up and keep the steam under it; give the dish a rounded bowl and a browned edge to the cheese; show the timer as eight lit green segments and four unlit, with no blink, so the still reads as "cooking"; enlarge "MIN LEFT".

## 181 The Wired Living Room

The scene reads at once: a window, a wooden cabinet television, a green sofa with four people, a rug and a cat, under a wall and a plank floor. Faults: the youngest figure's head covers the television menu, so "WEATHER" and "MAIL" are cut; the four figures are blocks, with no arms, so they look pinned to the sofa; the cat is a flat blob with no ears or face; the set-top terminal on the coffee table is too small to read as the object the concept names; the right wall is bare.

Changes planned: move the four people to the left, clear of the screen; add arms resting on the sofa arms; give the cat ears, closed eyes and a striped tail; enlarge the terminal and its keyboard; hang a framed picture on the right wall.

## 182 Neighbourhood Calendar, Maple Ridge Estates

Clean, legible and correct: the month grid, the day numbers, the four event blocks with short labels, the today box and the full legend all read at both sizes, and the cells draw a week at a time. It is the plainest page in the set and says little about the community it serves: every cell is the same, and nothing on the page says whose estate it is beyond the title. The rows are taller than the events need.

Changes planned: shorten the rows and use the space for a "COMING UP" panel with three dated events in full words; tint the Saturday and Sunday columns so the week has a shape; keep the labels legible.

## 183 The House That Orders Its Own Paper Towels

The best-realised concept for the medium: a cutaway house, a pantry with three shelves of stock, a low-stock flag that blinks from red to green, a terminal on a counter with a cable running to it, and a standing order on paper. Faults: the house is one flat face, so the cutaway has no depth; the roof is a plain tent; the sky is empty; the terminal's words are small; nothing shows the delivery that the order causes.

Changes planned: give the cutaway a darker interior band and a shadowed edge so the section reads as cut; redraw the roof as a gabled roof with eaves and a chimney; add a delivery van on the lawn, revealed last with WAIT; enlarge the terminal's text.

## 184 Home Watch

Clear and correct: each room is a flat fill with its name, the thermostat reads 68 F, the smoke detector is a disc in rings, the status panel lists five devices, and the porch lamp blinks. Faults: the plan has no doors or windows, so it reads as blocks rather than a floor plan; the thermostat is a label box, not a control; the porch lamp is small and sits on the wall line; the status dots carry no hierarchy.

Changes planned: add doors with swing arcs and windows in the outer walls; replace the thermostat box with a dial and needle; enlarge the porch lamp with a glow and label it.

## 185 Tonight's Horoscope

The wheel is the strongest image on the page: twelve segments, each its own palette entry, with the three-letter codes set in each, the hub reading TODAY, and the reader's sign (LEO) drawn white by blink. Faults: the reading lines (11 by 16) are small at 480 by 460; the upper-right of the panel and the lower-left corner are empty dark; the stars are too sparse to read as a night sky; the title is small and thin.

Changes planned: enlarge the reading to 12 by 18; add an outer row of planet markers and a crescent moon; add a "LUCKY COLOUR" row of four palette chips; draw a pointer from LEO to the reading.

## 186 School Bus Is Late

The headline does its job: ROUTE 14 IS LATE in large type on a red band, and the stop sign and the snowflakes place it in winter. Faults: the bus body is a box with a stray pale window at its rear-left and no visible front; its wheels are hidden under the snowbank; the lower half of the page is an empty white field; the hills band in the road colour reads as a grey wall, not a road; the snowflakes are few and small, so they read as stars.

Changes planned: put a road across the lower half with tyre tracks leading to the bus; redraw the bus front with a windscreen, a headlight and a door, and remove the stray window; set the snowflakes as a falling field of larger programmable characters.

## Summary of the round

Pages most in need of change: 178 (tulips and greeting) and 182 (plainness). Pages that already work: 179, 183 and 184, which change only in the detail named above. Two faults shared across the set are small body text at 480 by 460, and the static render of blinking elements, which shows one state only. The first is fixed by enlarging text in the pages whose lines are 11 or fewer units wide; the second by making each blink carry a state that is readable when still.
