# Round 1

Renders looked at: `rounds/round-0/`, every page at 640 × 480 and 480 × 460.

## Critique

**217 Chess by Wire.** The board is the strongest thing in the set so far: parchment and felt-green squares, programmable-character pieces with a four-pass outline that holds on both square colours, the last move flashing. The score panel is weaker. The "telephone wire" (two posts and a sagging arc) reads as a rope or a hammock, not as a line between two households, and it is the one place the page could say *by wire*. Nothing on the board shows what the opponent did beyond two orange squares. The foot line at 11 × 16 ("REPLY BY FRIDAY · 0 FOR GAMES INDEX") is the smallest text in the set and only just legible at 480 × 460. Changes: draw the two households and the line between them properly; draw the opponent's move as an arrow on the board; enlarge and shorten the foot line.

**218 The Maze.** Good depth from three graded wall colours and converging mortar; the torch and the glowing door are the right focal points. But the two side passages do not read: the left one looks like a recess and the right one is lost entirely, so the "4 LEFT / 6 RIGHT" prompt promises turnings the picture does not show. The ceiling is a flat purple with nothing to say it is a vault. There is no object of the game in view. Changes: make the openings read as passages (darker mouths, corner pillars, light falling off); vault the ceiling with ribs; put a key on the floor that glints by BLINK.

**219 Quiz Night.** Clear, bright and legible; the colour-map reveal is the right trick. But the lower third is thin: an empty time bar (it has drained by the end) and a pink "CORRECT" box that floats. The question card is all text. Changes: replace the bar with a round clock dial of sectors, which drains slice by slice and reads at a glance; draw a small globe on the question card so the question has a picture; give the reveal a proper banner.

**220 Gallery Page: Harbour at Dusk.** The strongest picture of the set: layered flat shapes, a sun in bands, glitter road, gabled town. Weaknesses: the hatched lighthouse beam renders as a striped wedge that looks like a fault rather than light; the outermost sky band ends in a visible circular edge at the top left, which reads as a planet; the town's lit windows have no reflection, so the water ignores the town. Changes: replace the hatched beam with two solid beams that alternate by BLINK (a sweep); widen and even out the sky bands so they read as a gradient; add window reflections and a few early stars.

**221 The Lace Picture.** A handsome medallion; the ring-by-ring reveal and the outward BLINK chase suit the medium. The corner fans are heavy, sunflower-like and cut off, and fight the lace. Between the bead ring and the scallops the lace has no bars, so the rings float instead of being joined, which is what makes lace lace. Changes: join the rings with fine radial bars; replace the corner fans with small lace corner pieces in ice and gold.

**222 Story Time: The Garden.** Charming and readable; the petals opening by SET COLOR is exactly the story-page idea. Wren is crude: a blue block with a head, no arms or legs to speak of, the can floating at her shoulder. Nothing comes to the flower once it opens. Changes: redraw Wren kneeling with a smock, arms and a hair bow; add a bee arriving after the flower opens; give the sun a face for a picture-book voice.

**223 The Chat Line.** Legible and believable as a screen; DRCS badges give each member an identity. But it is all list: name, line, name, line, with no sense that someone is talking to *you*. Changes: show a private message to the user as its own coloured panel with the heart beside it; put a night window with the moon in the members' column to set the hour; tighten the message lines so the private panel fits.

**224 The Face Maker.** Bright and fun; the face reads well and the menu highlight flashes. The face floats with no neck or shoulders, cut off at the chin above an empty backdrop; the chosen mouth is marked only by a thin outline that is nearly invisible at 480 × 460. Changes: add neck, collar and shoulders; mark the chosen thumbnail with a thick gold frame and number the three choices.

**225 Download Tonight's Program.** A good period scene. The digits on the arc collide and some overlap at both sizes; the cassette has no tape window; nothing says how long the family must wait. Changes: space the digits evenly along a gentler arc; give the cassette a window and tape; add a time-left line beside the progress.

**226 New Year's Eve 1990.** The fireworks and the lit skyline are lively, but the greeting set along the arc reads as scattered letters ("H A P P Y" climbing, "D E C A D E" falling), the magenta burst sits on the year, and the river does not reflect the fireworks. Changes: set the greeting as a straight banner on a ribbon above the year; move the bursts clear of the year and the greeting; add firework reflections in the river.

## Changes made

- **217:** the rope on the score panel became two red-roofed households with lit windows, a telephone line between them and an envelope (the move) on the line; the opponent's move d3–c4 is drawn on the board as a red arrow; the foot line is 12 × 18 and shortened to "REPLY BY FRIDAY · 0 INDEX".
- **218:** each side passage now has a shadowed facing wall, its own coursing and a corner pillar in the previous segment's wall colour, so both turnings read; ribs vault the ceiling at each depth; a key lies on the floor with a star that glints by BLINK (the page number and map moved off that entry to paper).
- **219:** the time bar became a round dial of ten sectors overdrawn slice by slice, ending at a pink 0; a globe (ocean, two continents, a shine) illustrates the question; the reveal is a full-width "RIGHT ANSWER: C" banner, and the right answer now flashes pink against green rather than going dark.
- **220:** the sky bands are ellipses 1.8 times wider than high round the sun, so they read as a gradient with the darkest sky in the corners; the hatched beam became two solid beams in their own entries that blink into the sky's colour in turn (a sweep); stars drawn in the windows' entry light with the town at dusk, and the windows are reflected under the pier.
- **221:** fine radial thread bars join the bead ring, lozenges, scallops and picot edge; the sunflower corners became small lace corners (ice scallops, cream beads and threads round a gold boss); the stray gold triangles that sat inside the lozenges (a rotation slip) are now small gold diamonds between them, on the bars.
- **222:** Wren redrawn kneeling, with a shoe, shin, skirt over the thigh, a smock with a collar, an arm reaching to the can, a bob with a pink bow, an eye, smile and cheek; a bee arrives at the open flower in a final WAIT step, its path dotted; the sun has a face.
- **223:** the last two public lines became a private message from Skye in its own lilac-bordered panel with the heart; the member column is tighter and ends in a night window with a crescent moon and stars; the typed reply answers the private message.
- **224:** shoulders in a green shirt, a neck and a white collar; the mouth thumbnails are numbered 4 to 6 to match "MOUTH 5", and the chosen one has a thick gold frame.
- **225:** the digits are eleven, evenly spaced on a gentler arc, none colliding; the cassette has tape packs on its reels and tape across its window; the band says how long is left.
- **226:** the scattered arc of letters became a straight greeting on a ribbon with pink notched tails; the bursts moved clear of the greeting and the year; each burst is reflected in the river as streaks of its own colour, so the reflections cycle with it; the tall tower moved and shortened, and the trails stop short of the year.
