# Round 14 · The Ember Tithe

## Critique

I inspected the only page, 176, at 480 × 460 and 640 × 480. The dragon and dark eclipse give the upper half two strong anchors. The fire leads naturally to the citadel, and the river turns the eye back into the valley. At 480 × 460 the keep's ledges and the receiving arch crowd together. At 640 × 480 the masonry separates more clearly, but the wide, similarly shaped pink flecks in the foreground water look like individual stepping stones. Their spacing and hard edges make the nearest water feel shallower than the valley suggests.

The plum-to-peach sky and blue-black banks establish distance and give the cream flame a focus. The strongest architectural light is concentrated at the landing. The channel above it is almost uniformly bright, while the dark wall directly beneath it gets little spill. The landing itself resembles a glowing shelf with no clear paving or change of plane. These two areas feel less finished than the dragon and corona.

NAPLPS works well here. Overlapping filled polygons carry the sky, mountain faces and wing membranes at both resolutions, and the 16-colour map ties together fire, sky and reflected light. Native arcs give the suspended coal a clean centre. Horizontal strokes are a good fit for water detail, though the heavy repeating polygons under the strokes are too regular.

The scene fits the brief as one large high-fantasy landscape with a clear event. A dragon and horned keep are familiar motifs. The invented ritual of delivering eclipse fire to a receiving arch and raising a coal above the keep gives them a specific relationship. The branching foreground trees and terraced forest help the world extend beyond the ritual.

There is no text in the 480 × 460 image. It contains no title, labels, numerals, lettering or credits to decipher. The title is recorded in the manifest.

## Priorities

1. Break the river reflection into staggered wave fronts, with widening dark intervals and finer glints that follow the changing current.
2. Give the receiving terrace a readable top and front face. Show the fire lighting the masonry along the route from the strike to the cradle.

## Changes made

- Replaced the repeated broad reflection patches with staggered wave faces, short peach crests and cooler broken ends. The highlights now stretch and separate as the river approaches the viewer.
- Cut visible joints into the receiving terrace, broke the firelight into facets along the fascia and added reflected light to the stone beneath the channel. Interrupted the brightest line in the channel so the gate strike and suspended coal remain the architectural focal points.
- Checked the generator and validator for stream-size limits. Both were already uncapped. The regenerated stream is 24,586 bytes, as reported in the manifest.
- Regenerated the stream, both PNGs, the contact sheet and the manifest. Inspected both native-size renders and the contact sheet, then refreshed the round 14 preview archive. A separate validation run passed, including the full reset, 7-bit stream and all 16 colour-map definitions. The lower gate and channel remain tightly spaced at 480 × 460, and the scene remains wordless.
