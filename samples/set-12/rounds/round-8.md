# Round 8 · The Ember Tithe

## Critique

I inspected page 176, the sole page, at 480 × 460 and 640 × 480. The composition works. The high wings balance the citadel, and the river gives the eye a route from the foreground into the basin. The eclipse, dragon's mouth and gate should form three distinct stages of the ritual, but the fire nearly grazes the lower corona and washes out against the peach sky. At 480 × 460 the eclipse is tall and the gate compresses into a patch of flame. At 640 × 480 the dragon reads more clearly, while the gaps in the fire's outline and the broad flat area of the foreground river become more conspicuous.

The warm sky and violet shadows set a coherent dusk palette with clear depth between the mountain ranges. The sky still has a striped quality, and the fire competes with those stripes. The river's broad violet interior bands follow its turns too neatly, like lanes on a road. The fine orange dashes suggest water, but share roughly the same weight from one bank to the other. Some of the small midground trees still read as serrations on the hillsides.

The scene uses NAPLPS well in the overlapping filled polygons of the wing membranes, cliffs and terraces. The native filled arcs make the suspended coal compact, and the small point and line accents hold up at both sizes. The fire and water would benefit from stronger silhouettes and more deliberate use of layered fills where thin lines are easy to lose.

The dragon, wooded basin and mountain citadel suit high fantasy. The receiving gate, recessed fire channel and split-spire cradle give the scene its own ritual and architecture. The dragon's bat wings and pointed background firs are familiar shapes. The scene contains zero visible words, letters, numerals, labels or credits at 480 × 460. The title appears in the manifest only. The 640 × 480 image is also wordless.

## Priorities

1. Separate the fire from the corona and give it a readable dark edge, a bright core and a clear impact inside the receiving arch.
2. Break the continuous near-water reflection into broad, staggered wave faces with narrower bright crests and pockets of dark water between them.
