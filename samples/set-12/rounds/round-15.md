# Round 15 · The Ember Tithe

## Critique

I inspected page 176, the set’s only page, at 480 × 460 and 640 × 480. The current outputs and the pre-existing round 15 snapshots differ, so I looked at both pairs and used the validated outputs as the editing baseline.

The eclipse and dragon hold the sky, the fire carries the eye into the keep, and the river leads it back from the lower edge. The warm sky against the blue-black banks gives the scene immediate depth. Peach shows up at roughly the same strength in the clouds, on the near wing and on some mountain faces, so those surfaces sometimes feel cut from the same material. The valley has a readable succession of ridges, but abrupt colour changes and a long, unbroken dark bank make parts of it look like stacked paper. At 640 × 480 the dark far wing has very little internal structure. At 480 × 460 its thin streak nearly disappears and the rear sail reads as a rigid blade.

Layered filled polygons give the mountains, wings and cliff clean silhouettes. The colour map makes firelight travel convincingly from the sky into the river, and the mix of filled shapes and short horizontal highlights gives the water a sense of motion. Native arcs work well for the coal cradled over the keep. The landing and receiving arch are more crowded. At 480 × 460 the star-shaped strike and the arch’s bright edge merge into a knot, leaving the dark opening hard to follow. The crenellated silhouette remains clear at both sizes.

The image fits the theme as one grand scene with an invented ritual visible in the action. The dragon, horned stronghold and eclipse are familiar fantasy forms. Their relationship is more distinctive: a living creature feeds fire into a structure built to receive it, and a suspended coal marks the far end of the exchange. The forked foreground trees add some local character, though their darkest limbs disappear against the nearest bank.

The 480 × 460 image contains no lettering, numerals, labels, title or credit. The title appears in the manifest.

## Priorities

1. Thread low mist through the distant valley to soften the joins between ridges and show which hills sit in front of others.
2. Give the far sail readable membrane folds and give the wing roots enough volume to show how they join the dragon.
3. Open up the receiving arch by concentrating the hottest part of the strike inside its throat.

## Changes made

- Added three low banks of mist at different depths. The far banks catch warm light in short patches, and the foreground ridge cuts in front of the nearest mist.
- Added shaded membrane folds and lit spars to the far wing, with a lit shoulder and a darker underside where the wings meet the body.
- Reduced the jagged flare at the receiving arch to a recessed pool of light. The opening stays dark around the impact, and the bright channel still leads up to the suspended coal.
- Audited the generator and validator. Neither contained a stream-size ceiling. The manifest records the new stream at 24,982 bytes.
- Regenerated the stream, both PNGs, the contact sheet and the manifest, and refreshed the round 15 snapshots. Visually checked both native sizes and the contact sheet. A separate validation run passed, including deterministic generation, the full reset, 7-bit encoding and all 16 colour-map definitions. The far wing remains subdued and the gate is still small at 480 × 460.
