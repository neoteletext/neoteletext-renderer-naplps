# Round 9 · The Ember Tithe

## Critique

Page 176 is the only page. I inspected the current renders at 480 × 460 and 640 × 480. The dragon and citadel balance across the basin, and the foreground river leads toward the exchange. The eclipse is a strong counterweight above the fire, though visibly taller at 480 × 460. The dark copper edge from round 8 keeps the fire readable over the peach sky. Its destination is less clear: the bright fill extends to the floor of the gate, flattening the arch and the impact into one shape at 480 × 460. The larger render exposes the same problem and makes the landing look like a glowing shelf.

Purple shadows and warm light distinguish the forest, cliffs and distant mountains. The sky is the weakest part of that depth progression. Long, nearly parallel edges read like broad flat stripes, especially to the left of the dragon, where the small clouds echo the bands rather than interrupting them. The river's broken highlights now read as water, though a dark smooth ribbon still follows the foreground bend. Some middle-distance trees merge into a sawtooth edge.

The layered NAPLPS fills are strongest where silhouettes overlap, particularly on the wings, river and cliff. The corona has an irregular outer profile and a clean dark disc. Filled shapes could do more to separate the air, stone and fire at the gate. Fine lines and points help describe embers and water, but alone are too small to establish those edges at 480 × 460.

This is a single high-fantasy scene with enough scale and atmosphere to connect the forest, mountains, dragon and citadel. The familiar wing shape and conifer silhouettes are less distinctive than the eclipse-fire delivery and the forked architecture that receives it. The setting and ritual are original to the picture. At 480 × 460 there are no visible words, letters, numerals, labels or credits to assess. The title is in the manifest. The 640 × 480 render is also wordless.

## Priorities

1. Give the dusk cloud banks distinct bodies, broken edges and light-facing undersides without adding clutter around the eclipse or fire.
2. Carve a dark recess into the receiving gate, give its frame and landing visible depth, and concentrate the firelight at the strike and on the surfaces facing it.

## Changes and checks

- Reshaped the broad dusk transitions and rebuilt the cloud banks with offset shadow masses and broken lit undersides. The clouds now have more individual silhouettes, though some broad horizontal palette steps remain visible.
- Rebuilt the receiving arch around a dark aperture, with a lit inner wall, visible stone joints and a sill that picks up the strike. Broke up the landing highlights with shadow facets. The opening and compact impact are separately visible at both sizes.
- Audited the generator and validator for stream size restrictions. Neither had a ceiling or size check. The manifest records the 20,144-byte stream.
- Regenerated the stream, both PNGs, the contact sheet and the manifest. The standalone validator passed deterministic generation, the initial full RESET, 7-bit encoding, all 16 palette definitions, clean rendering and byte-for-byte agreement with the saved artifacts.
- Inspected both regenerated sizes and the contact sheet. The page remains wordless. Saved the reviewed PNGs in [round-9](round-9/).
