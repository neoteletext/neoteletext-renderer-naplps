# Round 10 · The Ember Tithe

## Critique

I inspected page 176, the only page, at 480 × 460 and 640 × 480. The river's S-curve leads into the basin, and the dragon's breath carries the eye across to the lit gate. The eclipse anchors the upper right, though its tall oval is conspicuous at 480 × 460. The top of the picture has good breathing room. The foreground corners feel more like a frame than ground you could stand on.

The warm dusk and purple-black landforms establish depth. Near and far dragon wings use nearly the same copper and dark plum, weakening the overlap at the shoulder. At 480 × 460 the near wing's straight diagonal highlights compress into bands, and the long arm looks stiff at both sizes. The dark foreground banks have little depth within each silhouette, and their narrow, evenly spaced fir tips read as serration in the smaller render. The far sky still has a few nearly horizontal colour steps, and the river's centre has a dense patch of equally bright dashes near the bottom.

The best use of NAPLPS is in the filled, overlapping corona, layered rock and broad wing silhouettes. Their outlines survive both raster sizes. The tiny sparks and glints work as accents. The wings and framing woods could use more distinct, interlocking filled shapes that remain readable when the thin lines drop out.

The invented exchange of eclipse fire, the flume and the forked keep make the high-fantasy setting feel inhabited by a ritual. The dragon, citadel, snowy ridges and forest read as parts of one scene. The generic bat-wing anatomy and repeated conifer profiles do less to distinguish this world than the fire delivery and the coal held between the spires. At 480 × 460 there are no visible words, letters, numbers, labels or credits to assess. The title appears in the manifest, and the 640 × 480 render is also wordless.

## Priorities

1. Articulate the near wing with a visible wrist, separate membrane bays and colour changes across each fold. Recede the far wing so the shoulder overlap reads even at 480 × 460.
2. Turn the foreground silhouette into a wooded bank with irregular crowns, grounded trunks and exposed river-facing rock ledges. Keep the bright river's path open.
