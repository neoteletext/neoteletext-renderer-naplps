# Round 2 · The Ember Tithe

## Before

I inspected the single page, 176, at 480 × 460 and 640 × 480. The diagonal from the near wing through the dragon's muzzle to the receiving arch gives the upper half a clear direction. The river pulls the eye back into the scene, and the foreground firs give the valley scale. The brightest shape is the eclipse, though its wide, evenly spaced rings read like a target. At the narrower size it gets taller, and the roof tips almost merge with its lower rim. The cliff occupies a lot of space for a dark slab with parallel fractures. The river is brightest at the bottom, where several of its horizontal marks form a busy patch.

The plum shadows and peach light form a coherent palette, and the broad mountain silhouettes are easy to separate. The sky has much the same hard banding as the ridgelines. Most of its wisps share one stamp-like outline. The river's clipped reflections and the dragon's wing facets make good use of the flat palette, relative polygons and line commands. Native arcs give the eclipse a clean edge, but the circular geometry supplies almost all of its character. The wing tips and pointed architecture survive both render sizes. The tiny dragon eye and tower windows become pinpoints at 480 × 460.

A dragon, a mountain stronghold and a magical exchange clearly fit the single-scene high-fantasy theme. The world and its eclipse-fire tithe are invented. The five similar conical roofs look generic together, and the regularly spaced diamonds resemble a chain of projectiles. The gate reads as a small lit doorway, so it is easy to miss that the castle was built to receive the fire. There is no in-picture text at 480 × 460. The title appears in the supporting files.

## Priorities

1. Give the eclipse an irregular corona and clear space around the city silhouette. Use broken cloud layers to give the sky a different texture from the mountains.
2. Give the citadel distinctive eclipse-shaped architecture and make its receiving gate a substantial part of the rock and terrace.
3. Draw the tithe as a continuous, curling flow of fire with a visible arrival at the gate.

## Changes and checks

- Moved the eclipse up and left, separating the corona from the roofline. Shaped its uneven streamers with filled polygons, retained the native arcs for its core and added cloud banks that cross the sky bands.
- Replaced the cluster of spires with a split keep that cradles a suspended coal. Enlarged the receiving arch and built it into the terrace with splayed piers, visible steps and a brighter interior.
- Replaced the evenly spaced fire diamonds with a tapered ribbon that curls into the arch. Short offshoots and scattered sparks follow the ribbon.
- Confirmed that the generator and validator already have no byte-size ceiling. Regenerated the 10,566-byte stream, both PNGs, the contact sheet and the manifest. The validator passed deterministic generation, full RESET, the 7-bit check, all 16 colour definitions, clean rendering and artifact comparison.
- Inspected both final renders and the contact sheet. The split keep and the path into the receiving arch read at both sizes. The eclipse is still taller at 480 × 460, and the foreground river still carries a dense patch of highlights. The scene remains wordless.
