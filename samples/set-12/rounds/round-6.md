# Round 6 · The Ember Tithe

## Critique

The sole page has a strong triangular composition. The dragon leads into the receiving arch, the eclipse anchors the upper right, and the widening river returns the eye to the foreground. At 640 × 480 the river and cliff have room to spread. At 480 × 460 the eclipse looks tall and the dragon's wing panels compress. At both sizes the near wing tip grazes a dark cloud streak, and the wing reads as a narrow strip with two abrupt notches. The eye and hooked talons survive at the smaller size.

Apricot firelight against plum and blue-black makes the dragon and the citadel easy to locate. The water ripples carry the warmth into the foreground, but the salmon bands behind the river flatten the mountain basin. Several run nearly the full width at the same weight. The castle's orange doorway stands out clearly, though its landing ends in a jagged bright lip and the lower wall looks pasted onto the cliff.

Relative polygon fills give the cliffs, trees and wings strong silhouettes, and the sparse point and line marks keep the sparks and water crisp. The repeated horizontal contours use the medium less well. They have few overlaps that explain which hill or bank is in front. The coal remains visible at both sizes, though the brightest part of its feed channel reads as a single fine stroke at 480 × 460.

The dragon, pointed roofs and eclipse are familiar high-fantasy motifs. The split keep cradling a coal fed by a dragon gives them a specific invented purpose within one continuous scene. The two outer towers are comparatively generic. The image contains no visible title, captions, labels, numerals or other text at 480 × 460 or 640 × 480.

## Priorities

1. Give the near wing a broader membrane with connected spars and pull its leading tip clear of the dark cloud.
2. Break the foothill stripes into overlapping ridges and let local mist follow the hollows.
3. Build a visible foundation under the receiving landing so the gate and its terrace feel anchored to the cliff.

## Changes and checks

- Lowered and broadened the near wing and redrew its membranes with tapered spars running into the shoulder. The warm leading edge separates from the cloud at both sizes.
- Replaced the long salmon bands with overlapping foothills, lit shoulders and pockets of mist. The ridges now frame the head of the river and read as distinct slopes.
- Gave the receiving arch a projecting stone landing and stepped piers keyed into the cliff. The lit cap and shadowed underside both hold at 480 × 460.
- Reviewed the rebuilt PNGs at both native sizes and the contact sheet and archived them in [round-6](round-6/). The page is wordless. The shared eclipse outline still appears taller at 480 × 460 than at 640 × 480.
