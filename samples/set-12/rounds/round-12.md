# Round 12 · The Ember Tithe

## Critique

I inspected page 176, the only page, at 480 × 460 and 640 × 480. The dragon and eclipse hold opposite sides of the sky, and the fire and S-shaped river bring the eye down through the keep and into the foreground. Plum shadows give the dragon a readable silhouette against the peach sky. The warm-to-cool shift does much of the work of placing the mountains behind the dark banks. The eclipse still reads a little taller at 480 × 460 and a little wider at 640 × 480. The broader render makes the mountain layers easier to separate.

The foreground water is the weakest part of the depth cue. At 480 × 460 the orange dashes merge into a noisy patch at the foot of the frame. At 640 × 480 their separation exposes how evenly the short strokes are scattered. The longer plates of colour underneath are too regularly spaced to suggest water turning through a bend. At the keep, the glowing flume now reads as a continuous route, but the broad stone faces beneath the strike are nearly as cool and dark as rock much farther away. Several look like disconnected angular fragments because their edges have little shared light.

The overlapping filled polygons suit NAPLPS. The dragon's membranes and stratified cliff read through silhouette and occlusion, and the indexed colours tie the sky, water and masonry together. The circular arc used for the suspended coal gives it a clean bright core. Fine lines and points work for sparks where they are sparse. The foreground reflection leans too hard on the same thin marks where larger wave shapes should establish distance and current.

The scene fits the high-fantasy brief through a single landscape and a single action. The dragon delivers eclipse fire to an invented keep, with the receiving arch, flume and forked cradle giving the ritual a physical route. The eclipse tithe and the split-spire architecture give the world its strongest identity. The bat-winged dragon, conventional firs and serrated peaks are familiar fantasy forms, even with original geometry. The scene feels most distinctive where the landscape and the ritual visibly affect each other.

At 480 × 460 there is no text to read. The scene has no labels, letters, numerals or credits. “The Ember Tithe” appears in the manifest, outside both renders.

## Priorities

1. Give the river a perspective hierarchy using broad, interrupted wave faces near the viewer, narrower bands up the bends, and quieter water between them.
2. Carry the strike light across the landing and onto the upper cliff so the masonry visibly belongs to the same event and the same rock face.

## Changes made

- Replaced the dense scatter of bright foreground dashes with tapered wave faces, broken crests, and quieter side currents. The reflections narrow toward the distant bend and alternate in brightness through the foreground.
- Extended the receiving strike's warm light across the landing, over the terrace lip and onto the nearest exposed cliff ledges. Lower faces fall back into shadow.
- Checked the generator and validator for a stream-size ceiling. Both already accept streams without a byte cap. The manifest records the byte count, and validation still checks the 7-bit encoding, initial full reset and all 16 colour definitions.
- Regenerated the stream, both PNGs, the contact sheet and the manifest. Inspected both final renders and the contact sheet, refreshed the round 12 preview archive, and ran `validate.py` successfully. Both renders remain wordless.
