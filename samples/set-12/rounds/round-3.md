# Round 3 · The Ember Tithe

## Before

I inspected page 176 at 480 × 460 and 640 × 480. The dragon, fire and gate make an easy left-to-right read. The low river bend and framing firs establish depth, but the nearest water is crowded with evenly spaced bright scratches. Its hard banks and broad salmon fill make it look like a strip of metal. The dark cliff dominates the right side below the keep. Its near-parallel streaks give little sense of ledges or rock strata, so the castle appears perched on a flat cutout.

The warm peach horizon and cool plum silhouettes separate the planes well. The cream of the eclipse and the gate commands attention. The dragon's elbow, legs and jaw merge into the body at 480 × 460. Even at 640 × 480 the stiff near wing and small squared head read more readily than the animal's banking posture. The eclipse is visibly taller at the narrower size, and its broad scallops resemble a decorative border.

The solid silhouettes, layered polygon facets and clean native arc exploit the limited palette well. The sparks and short line strokes are a good use of native point and line commands, though the uniform ripple spacing calls attention to the drawing technique. The combined dragon, eclipse and fire-collecting stronghold fit the high-fantasy brief as a single scene. The invented tithe and split keep give it an identity, while the bat wing, flanking turrets and repeated arcade openings feel more familiar. There is no text in the picture at 480 × 460. The title exists in the supporting files.

## Priorities

1. Give the river distinct currents and reflected light that tightens into the distance.
2. Build the stronghold into layered rock with deep shadow and firelight falling from the gate.
3. Clarify the dragon's banking anatomy and give its head and wing joints identifiable shapes at the smaller size.

## Changes and checks

- Rebuilt the river as a warm, winding reflection within cooler water. Varied the lengths, spacing and colours of the bank-clipped wave crests with depth, leaving dark water visible at the foreground bend.
- Replaced the cliff's parallel fractures with layered ledges and recessed seams. Added warm stone facets directly below the receiving gate and kept the lower rock in shadow.
- Articulated the dragon's near wing joint, added throat plates and a lit brow, and redrew the talons so they are visible against the sky and valley.
- Confirmed that the generator and validator already impose no stream byte ceiling. Regenerated the 11,337-byte stream, both native-size PNGs, the contact sheet and the manifest. The validator passed deterministic generation, the full reset, 7-bit encoding, all 16 colour definitions, clean rendering and artifact comparison.
- Inspected both final renders and the contact sheet and saved the previews for this round. The foreclaw and the firelit ledges read at both sizes. Individual throat plates are still fine at 480 × 460, and the eclipse retains its taller appearance in that viewport. The picture is wordless.
