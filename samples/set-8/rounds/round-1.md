# Set 8 · Improvement round 1

## Critique and priorities

I compared each full-size 480 × 460 render with its 640 × 480 counterpart. The narrower raster compresses the horizontal geometry and letterforms. The following text checks are for 480 × 460.

### 136 · Light That Stays

Cyan and amber separate the outcomes immediately against the cobalt slab, but the amber reflection nearly runs into the right edge. The dashed normals and staged ray reveal use NAPLPS well. The subject fits physics directly, although the two-path textbook illustration is familiar. Title, TOTAL INTERNAL REFLECTION, AIR, GLASS, BELOW CRITICAL and ABOVE CRITICAL all read. The tiny arcs look like stray marks and the thin cyan reflection could be mistaken for a guide.

**Priorities** — Put numerical incidence and critical angles on the plate. Give both paths comparable clearance and distinguish the weaker partial reflection.

### 137 · The Still Points

The three lobes make a good horizontal composition, with lime markers clearly above the purple grid. At both sizes, the bright lobes appear to alternate sides along one profile, so the palette blink conveys motion unusually economically. The intermediate profiles muddy the picture and look like extra strings, especially at channel size. Title, THIRD HARMONIC, both NODE labels, FIXED ENDS and TWO EXTREMES read, but the latter two float below their referents. Strong physics fit and a distinctive animated treatment.

**Priorities** — Make the equilibrium and the two extremes visually distinct. Show where the amplitude peaks and tie the fixed supports into the same labeling scheme.

### 138 · Salt in Three Dimensions

The ivory and mint split organizes the two views, and the coral and teal ions are easy to distinguish. Indexed discs and depth ordering suit NAPLPS, but uniform dark connectors make the cube look like a shelving unit at both sizes. The inset is a useful crystallography subject and the two-view treatment adds interest to a familiar model. Both title lines, ROCK SALT, Na+, Cl- and both lines of 6 NEAREST NEIGHBOURS read. The inset's hairlines nearly disappear and its projection feels disconnected from the main model.

**Priorities** — Separate the cell boundary from internal site links and emphasize foreground depth. Draw a clearer coordination inset from the same lattice axes.

### 139 · Ink in Flight

The dark sidebar balances the narrow paper strip. The pigments have excellent contrast, but the three hard lumps resemble torn paper at both resolutions. Native bath hatching and WAIT are good uses of the medium. Paper chromatography fits chemistry well, with a familiar apparatus and underused opportunities for a more distinctive plate. INK IN FLIGHT, PAPER, CHROMATOGRAPHY, DYES TRAVEL / AT DIFFERENT / RATES, SOLVENT FRONT, FLOW, ORIGIN and SOLVENT all read. CHROMATOGRAPHY is the smallest and tightest label.

**Priorities** — Show the initial spot and developed strip together in the final frame. Render soft-edged pigment bands and an unambiguous baseline and solvent front.

### 140 · A Leaf Breathes

The luminous pair has immediate focus and both leaders reach their targets. Title, GUARD CELLS and OPEN PORE read cleanly. The mechanically mirrored dots, sharp pavement seams and small crossing flecks at either pole make the anatomy feel unfinished at both sizes. Contour fills suit NAPLPS and the microscopy view fits plant biology. The negative-space pore is the most original compositional choice.

**Priorities** — Redraw the guard-cell walls and pore with clean junctions and differentiated anatomy. Replace angular seams with an interlocking, lobed epidermis.

### 141 · One Cell Becomes Two

The plum, lilac and rose palette is coherent, but a row of four small specimens leaves too little room for spindle detail. The relative chromosome macro is effective. The subject fits cell biology squarely and the fluorescence treatment gives a familiar sequence a point of view. Title, MITOSIS IN AN ANIMAL CELL, PROPHASE, METAPHASE, ANAPHASE, TELOPHASE and SISTER CHROMATIDS SEPARATE read. Fine spindle lines are hard to follow and the last stage loses the visual count of chromosomes.

**Priorities** — Give each stage a larger field and make sequence direction explicit. Preserve the four-chromosome example through telophase and visibly distinguish poles, fibres and chromatids.

### 142 · A Star Winks

Gold against navy gives the star strong presence and cyan separates the curve. The two halves sit next to each other without a visible correspondence, and the channel raster elongates the discs. Computed polylines and clean disc overpainting are good NAPLPS choices. The quantitative transit is a strong astronomy fit, though the arrangement is standard. Title, EXOPLANET TRANSIT, RELATIVE LIGHT, PLANET, TIME and DIP: ABOUT 2.6% read. The near-invisible axes and unnumbered grid make the graph look more precise than it can be read.

**Priorities** — Identify the high and low flux levels directly. Link the silhouettes of the crossing planet to positions on the light curve.

### 143 · The Ocean Keeps a Record

The muted rust and blue block is a good focal point and the mirrored widths carry the earth-science idea. At both sizes the dense hatch reads as a screen texture, and the block looks more like a striped roof than newly formed ocean crust. Native fills and oblique faces suit it. The title gives a familiar geology model a distinctive framing. Both title lines, MAGNETIC STRIPES, RIDGE AXIS, NORMAL and REVERSED read clearly.

**Priorities** — Expose a crustal section under the ridge and connect it to outward spreading. Replace dense hatching with marks that survive both resolutions and make symmetry easy to trace.

### 144 · Stone That Shrinks

The slate against apricot is handsome, but the cliff reads as a free-standing cluster of manufactured pencils. The front tips almost hit the caption band and tiny horizontal gaps appear between some top faces. NAPLPS flat facets work well and the geology subject fits, although the illustration does little to explain contraction or distinguish a joint from an isolated prism. Title, COLUMNAR BASALT and COOLING LAVA CONTRACTS AND CRACKS read. The warm background silhouettes are too generic to situate the rock.

**Priorities** — Build a contiguous cliff with a shared foot and stratification. Add a plan view that makes the shared fracture network clear.

### 145 · A Square Moves

The two balanced squares and vivid blue uncovered regions make this the clearest composition in the group. Polygon fills and the WAIT exploit NAPLPS well. This is a classic mathematical proof, with originality mainly in its pacing and print palette. Title, PYTHAGORAS BY REARRANGEMENT, c², a², b² and SAME TRIANGLES. SAME AREA. all read. The small superscripts hold up, but the two warm fills each identify two different triangles. Neither the leg lengths nor the final equation is stated.

**Priorities** — Give each triangle a unique visual identity that persists across arrangements. Label the legs and make the area equality explicit.

## Changes made

- **136 · Light That Stays** — Added the incidence and critical angles, strengthened the angle arcs, gave the amber path more clearance, and labeled the weaker partial reflection in its own tint.
- **137 · The Still Points** — Removed the ghost profiles, keyed the rest line and the two blinking extremes, and marked the fixed endpoints and the three positions of maximum motion.
- **138 · Salt in Three Dimensions** — Established a hierarchy from the front perimeter to the inner links, shaded ions by depth, and redrew the octahedron on the same oblique axes with spokes to its central sodium ion.
- **139 · Ink in Flight** — Preserved the starting spot and revealed a second strip beside it. Layered pigment contours show the separated components against a common origin and a marked solvent front.
- **140 · A Leaf Breathes** — Rebuilt the pore, thickened the facing walls, separated the cells at their poles, and replaced angular seams and mirrored dots with lobed pavement walls and varied organelle placement.
- **141 · One Cell Becomes Two** — Rebuilt the sequence as a numbered two-by-two plate, differentiated poles, fibres and chromatids, and showed four chromosome marks in each re-forming nucleus.
- **142 · A Star Winks** — Aligned five planet positions to the curve and labeled the 100% baseline, 97.44% minimum and 2.56% dip.
- **143 · The Ocean Keeps a Record** — Cut through the ridge to expose the basalt and underlying material, marked where new basalt forms, and replaced the dense reversal hatch with spaced strokes.
- **144 · Stone That Shrinks** — Joined the columns to a common base, added cross-fractures to the exposed faces, and included a plan view of shared joints.
- **145 · A Square Moves** — Gave all four triangles persistent colours and letters, labeled the legs and added the final area equation.
