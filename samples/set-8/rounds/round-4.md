# Round 4 · Science in NAPLPS

I inspected all ten incoming pages at 480 × 460 and 640 × 480. The text checks below refer to the incoming 480 × 460 renders, with superscripts transcribed as such.

## Incoming critique and priorities

### 136 · Light That Stays

The cyan and amber rays separate the cases instantly, and the dark air above cobalt glass is a strong two-tone composition. Native arcs and the delayed ray reveal fit the subject especially well. The side-by-side optical experiment is more distinctive than either ray diagram on its own. LIGHT THAT STAYS, TOTAL INTERNAL REFLECTION, AIR, GLASS, GLASS TO AIR, CRITICAL 42 DEG, 39 DEG REFRACTED, PARTIAL REFLECTION, TOTAL REFLECTION and both footer captions read. BELOW and ABOVE depend on the reader finding the critical-angle note. The large right-hand reflected ray looks unfinished with its angle marked on only one side.

- Mark the incoming and outgoing angles of the amber case with paired arcs.
- Separate the two experiments and tie the lower captions directly to the condition for reflection.

### 137 · The Still Points

The purple traces and lime nodes make the physics readable immediately. Palette animation is particularly apt for alternating the two extremes of an oscillation. The oscilloscope treatment gives a stock standing-wave diagram some character. THE STILL POINTS, THIRD HARMONIC, ANTINODES, ONE WAVELENGTH, both FIXED and NODE pairs, TWO EXTREMES and REST POSITION all read. The arrows can suggest that only the three peak points move. The long end bars look like two more peaks, while the outer nodes resemble the inner ones despite their different boundary condition.

- Show intermediate string profiles to explain motion between extremes.
- Replace the long end bars with visible clamps and distinguish the interior nodes.

### 138 · Salt in Three Dimensions

Ivory and cool teal feel like a mineral atlas, and depth-ordered circles make the repetitive rock-salt lattice tractable. Enlarging one local neighbourhood is a useful original composition for standard crystallography. SALT IN THREE DIMENSIONS, ROCK SALT, Na+, Cl- and 6 NEAREST NEIGHBOURS are legible. The inset runs directly from the legend into the illustration without a cue that it enlarges the selected group. A black rectangular front frame dominates the lattice, and many corners appear to float on almost invisible edges.

- Explain which ion is at the centre and connect the highlighted group visually to the coordination inset.
- Make the unit-cell boundary read as a consistent spatial box while keeping the six bonds dominant.

### 139 · Ink in Flight

The warm paper and dark sidebar make an attractive laboratory print. Native bath hatch, WAIT and the nested contours make a familiar chemistry demonstration feel handmade. INK IN FLIGHT, PAPER CHROMATOGRAPHY, DYE TRAVEL, DIVIDED BY, SOLVENT TRAVEL, MEASURE FROM, THE ORIGIN, START, DEVELOPED, SOLVENT FRONT, ORIGIN, BATH and 0.84, 0.54 and 0.26 are readable. The sidebar repeats the origin annotation and never names the quantity. The long thin measurement traces resemble wires where they share the origin, and the three spots look like undifferentiated blobs.

- Name the retention factor and show a worked calculation with distances taken from the diagram.
- Turn the measurement traces into independent brackets and add distinctive pigment shapes.

### 140 · A Leaf Breathes

The leaf-green specimens and winding cell seams evoke a microscope. Knockout fills and nested contours are good uses of the medium. The open/closed comparison is clear biology, though identical background tessellations and near-identical organelle patterns make the images feel stamped. The title, subtitle, both pore states, both turgor labels, GUARD CELL and NUCLEUS are individually readable. The lower M-shaped mass of labels is awkward. The anatomical leaders belong to different panels, so neither panel gives a complete reading, and the closed image has little evidence of a thick inner wall.

- Give the states and the anatomical key separate space and group the anatomy on the open specimen.
- Draw the facing thick walls explicitly and make the two epidermal fields distinct.

### 141 · One Cell Becomes Two

The four panels, large chromosome marks and clockwise reading order make a recognizable fluorescence-style biology plate. The repeated X macro and consistently tracked palette make the medium do real work. The title, subtitle, numbers 1–4 and PROPHASE, METAPHASE, ANAPHASE and TELOPHASE are readable. Colour tracking is more distinctive than the conventional phase grid. The finished-looking outline in the last panel is still one pinched cell, and the prophase inset gives no hint that the spindle begins to form.

- Add explicit stage actions and show the early spindle poles.
- Rework the last panel to show nuclear re-formation alongside near-complete cytokinesis.

### 142 · A Star Winks

Amber against deep navy is striking. The aligned graph and planet silhouettes are an original way to connect geometry and photometry, and native circles plus a vector light curve suit the subject. A STAR WINKS, EXOPLANET TRANSIT, ONE PLANET, FOUR POSITIONS, RELATIVE LIGHT, 100%, 97.44%, 2.56% DIP, INGRESS, EGRESS and TIME read. The horizontal percentage guides float without a y-axis. The flat bottom looks overly schematic because the page never says it models a uniform star. The four silhouettes still have equal weight and can read as separate planets.

- Give the graph a real vertical scale and state the uniform-star assumption on the page.
- Number and differentiate the four contact snapshots, matching them to the plotted events.

### 143 · The Ocean Keeps a Record

The marine blue and rust block has a strong silhouette and uses solid polygon bands and line texture to carry polarity. The layered oblique cutaway is a distinctive fit for earth science. Both title lines, MAGNETIC STRIPES, SAME AGE, RIDGE AXIS, both SPREADING labels, RISING MELT, NORMAL and REVERSED read. The same-age leaders cross the ridge annotation at a busy crest. The example lands on a high-contrast orange band and leaves the reader to infer the full symmetry, and the repeated spreading captions consume room that could explain age.

- Make the mirrored sequence and outward age gradient explicit.
- Route the ridge and sample annotations independently and centre the reversed texture within each stripe.

### 144 · Stone That Shrinks

The juncture of apricot landscape and slate rock makes the geological process unusually tangible. Reprojecting the same joint network for both views is original and a particularly good use of native polygons. The title, COLUMNAR BASALT, COOLING FACE, JOINT GROWTH, TOP VIEW, JOINTS and footer read. The repeated horizontal marks still look painted on because they ignore the orientation of the facets. One pale cap appears in both views, but the eye has to infer the correspondence and the column beneath the highlight looks interchangeable with the others.

- Map short, branching fracture marks onto the exposed facets.
- Carry the highlighted cap down one column and explicitly identify the matching feature in the plan.

### 145 · A Square Moves

Ultramarine and four warm/cool triangle colours make area conservation immediately visible. Flat polygons and WAIT are exactly right for a rearrangement. The proof is established mathematics and the visual treatment is original. The title, subtitle, both a+b brackets, both sets of A–D, a, b and c, c², a² and b², MOVE SAME FOUR, EQUAL UNCOVERED AREAS and the final equation are readable. The oblique blue region is asserted to be a square without accounting for its corners. The transfer annotation also leaves the conserved outer area implicit.

- Mark the hypotenuse square corners and show why their angles are right.
- State the two conserved areas directly so the concluding equality follows from the visual.
