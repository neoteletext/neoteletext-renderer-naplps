# Round 3 · Science in NAPLPS

## Before editing

I inspected each current output in a native-size pair, 480 × 460 on the left and 640 × 480 on the right. The narrower raster elongates the circular specimens and compresses lateral gaps, particularly around the transit contacts and the salt lattice.

### 136 · Light That Stays
The cobalt slab and saturated rays make a strong optical diagram. Dashed normals, native angle arcs and the WAIT reveal make good use of the format. The side-by-side threshold experiment gives a familiar textbook subject a clear visual premise. At 480, the title, subtitle, AIR, GLASS, GLASS TO AIR, CRITICAL ANGLE 42 DEG, REFRACTED, PARTIAL REFLECTION, TOTAL REFLECTION and both angle captions are readable. The weak reflected ray is suitably subdued, but its label is weaker than necessary. The bend is visible without a corresponding angle in air, and the two ray families nearly touch at the foot of the slab.
- Annotate the angle in air with a matching arc.
- Move the below-critical experiment left enough to give both ray families breathing room and brighten the secondary label.

### 137 · The Still Points
The alternating lilac profiles and green nodes make a good oscilloscope-like physics plate. Palette blink lets the linework carry time without redrawing. The presentation is a conventional harmonic plot with a lively execution. The title, THIRD HARMONIC, ANTINODES, both FIXED and NODE labels, TWO EXTREMES and REST POSITION are readable at 480. The antinode leader only touches one crest and the dark travel arrows look more like incidental grid marks.
- Give all three antinodes equal treatment and improve displacement-arrow contrast.
- Show the wavelength directly on the plot to connect the three lobes to the harmonic.

### 138 · Salt in Three Dimensions
Ivory, teal and coral give the dense lattice a clear mineral-plate look. Depth-ordered native circles and a shaded polyhedron suit NAPLPS. The coordination inset and the unit cell make a good chemistry pairing, though ball-and-stick imagery is familiar. Both title lines, ROCK SALT, Na+, Cl- and both lines of 6 NEAREST NEIGHBOURS are readable at 480. Gold ties get lost among edges, and the inset's rear ion looks detached because the depth edges are barely drawn.
- Distinguish construction lines from coordination links more strongly.
- Complete the rear wireframe and reuse the lattice's gold highlight in a centred coordination inset.

### 139 · Ink in Flight
The split lab-print layout, nested ink contours and native bath hatch have personality. WAIT turns a standard separation diagram into a small experiment. INK IN FLIGHT, PAPER CHROMATOGRAPHY, FRACTION OF SOLVENT TRAVEL, all three decimal values, START, DEVELOPED, SOLVENT FRONT, ORIGIN, BATH and FLOW are readable at 480. The blank start strip is appropriate but the separated values sit far from the marks they measure. The ruler's unlabelled internal ticks communicate little.
- Put each fraction beside the corresponding spot and show the distance from the common origin.
- Use the sidebar to explain how the ratio is formed.

### 140 · A Leaf Breathes
The pair of green fields and inked pavement seams read as a stylized microscope plate. Layered contours, colour indexing and a knockout aperture make the biology recognizable. The matched views are effective, although duplicated seam patterns make them feel mechanically copied. At 480 the title, subtitle, OPEN PORE, CLOSED PORE, HIGH TURGOR and LOW TURGOR read clearly. GUARD CELL is readable but competes with the seams and its leader ends on a chloroplast. The shut aperture still looks like a thin cutout.
- Draw the closed contact seam without an underlying void.
- Move anatomical labels out of the specimens and point explicitly to a guard cell and its nucleus.

### 141 · One Cell Becomes Two
Four rose-and-lilac fields make a clear animal-cell sequence, with clockwise arrows and a reusable relative chromosome macro. Native discs and indexed overlays evoke fluorescence, though the repeated pink X shapes look generic. The title, subtitle, numerals 1–4 and all four phase names are clear at 480. The spindles are prominent but give no way to trace individual chromosomes, and the cleavage arrows are too small to explain the final shape.
- Track four chromosomes by hue from condensation through separation and nuclear reformation.
- Improve the stage fields and emphasize the narrowing cleavage furrow.

### 142 · A Star Winks
Amber and ice blue give the astronomy plate excellent contrast. Native circles and a computed curve share one horizontal alignment, an effective original synthesis of two familiar diagrams. At 480 the title, subtitle, TRANSIT CONTACTS, RELATIVE LIGHT, four contact numbers, both percentage ticks, TIME and 2.56% DIP are readable. Closely paired planets look like multiple bodies, and the curve lacks a clear indication of which intervals are ingress and egress.
- Explicitly identify the silhouettes as snapshots of one planet.
- Trade the distant contact numbers for an aligned interval key beneath the trace, retaining the calculated contact points.

### 143 · The Ocean Keeps a Record
The rust and marine-blue block is a crisp and distinctive earth-science plate. Oblique polygons and short strokes carry polarity even without colour. The two title lines, MAGNETIC STRIPES, SAME AGE, RIDGE AXIS, both SPREADING labels, RISING MELT, NORMAL and REVERSED read at 480. The leaders compete around the ridge crest. The cutaway's single flat mantle fill and cropped melt triangle leave the cross-section looking shallow and unfinished.
- Give the cut face enough depth for a layered melt pathway and directional arrows.
- Mark the matching stripes with a high-contrast paired treatment that survives the patterned fill.

### 144 · Stone That Shrinks
Slate columns against a warm field give the geology plate a strong silhouette. Shared irregular joints between plan and oblique views are an original and convincing use of vector geometry. At 480 the title, COLUMNAR BASALT, COOLING FACE, JOINT GROWTH, TOP VIEW, JOINTS and the footer all read. JOINTS nearly joins its leader. The rectangular inset cuts through the cliff at right and the thin, repeated horizontal ripples look like ruled lines.
- Fit the cliff and plan into distinct spaces without clipping the columns.
- Vary the fracture lengths and retain the matched highlight in both views.

### 145 · A Square Moves
The two vividly coloured square dissections make an excellent mathematical use of NAPLPS flat fills and WAIT. The proof is familiar, with good original colour and typography choices. At 480, the title, subtitle, both a+b brackets, A–D in both squares, a, b, c, all three square-area labels, the caption and the final equation read. The tiny tick for c looks stray, b is stranded against the left margin, and the anonymous connecting arrow glosses over what stays equal.
- Use clear dimension marks on all three sides of one triangle.
- Clarify that the same four triangles are moved and mark the shape of the remaining areas.
