# Set 11 · Round 2 of 4

I inspected each incoming PNG at 480 × 460 and 640 × 480. Each image contains one title. The legibility notes below refer to the 480 × 460 render.

## 166 · Keelward

The apricot and cyan windows read well against navy, but the similarly lit towers still form a curtain of teeth. The vacant maroon ceiling and flat violet bands squander the depth promised by the inverted salt-moon city. The city is a distinctive setting, and filled facets and rectangular windows are a good match for NAPLPS. The yellow zigzag looks painted across the towers, while the tiny ferry could be mistaken for a loose stroke. KEELWARD is readable, though the narrow W looks crowded.

- Cut visible fractures and salt beds into the ceiling and key the main tower into them.
- Turn the zigzag into a supported transit route with a carriage and station.
- Widen the title lettering slightly.

## 167 · Cinder Mail

The cream sail makes a good diagonal across the warm ash bands, with teal giving the tiny courier a clear colour identity. NAPLPS facets are a natural match for the sail, but its taut triangular silhouette reads as a kite, and its lower red panels merge with the ash. The courier in a stellar ash current fits the theme, but the setting needs a physical interaction to distinguish it from an ordinary solar sail. The three little curves and groups of identical dots read like bits of decoration. CINDER MAIL is clear, including the thin strokes of the I.

- Shape the ash into a diverted flow around the sail.
- Give the lower sail structural ribs and make its rigging terminate visibly on the ship.

## 168 · Prism Grazer

The acid-yellow sky and teal-violet facets give the animal a strong silhouette, though the flat yellow field and angular shadow make it feel like a sticker. NAPLPS polygons convincingly suggest cut glass. A creature that eats daylight is one of the most original ideas here, but the dark needle of a muzzle barely touches the glowing seam and the path into the body looks like a decorative circuit. PRISM GRAZER reads clearly in dark blue.

- Make the feeding contact visible as a split ray that enters the throat and refracts through the body.
- Give the seam excavated faces and cast fractured coloured light under the animal.

## 169 · The Second Floor

The blue and peach perspectives explain opposite gravity quickly. The nested frames use NAPLPS lines and polygon fills well, but the corridor is bare and the two cloned figures look like pictograms with rubber arms. A meeting across two floors is a strong science-fiction moment despite the familiar gravity reversal premise. THE SECOND FLOOR reads end to end. Its dark slab crops the lower landing's edge.

- Turn the opposing landings into convincing survey platforms with visible deck and edge construction.
- Distinguish the two suits and give the handoff a readable tool and grip.
- Fit the title slab nearer the actual title width.

## 170 · Umbra Loom

The rose star limb and staggered diagonal of shuttles make a strong silhouette against aubergine. Native circles and the animated highlight suit the medium. The original idea is weaving a stellar resource, yet each straight-edged bundle resembles a multicolour cable dragging behind a matching purple dart. The cropped star's interior is blank and the ribbon sources look like pins driven into a ring. UMBRA LOOM reads clearly.

- Build hooked eruptions at the ribbon sources and bend the strands into visible twists.
- Give each shuttle a mouth with a visible guide mechanism, preserving BLINK.

## 171 · Rainworks

Copper vessels stand out against the sea-green frame, but five cloned bays and synchronised strings of identical diamonds make the weather foundry look like a screen test. The repeating drop macro and WAIT are apt NAPLPS tools and the invented idea fits the theme. The water below looks like angular hills with floating hyphens. RAINWORKS is clear with generous space around it.

- Show different vessel pressures and a larger main discharge.
- Vary drop trajectories and stage the impacts as overlapping ripples on a catchment.

## 172 · Choir of Ice

Cobalt walls and icy spires are a natural palette, with peach notes easy to spot. The hatch gives the reeds a real material distinction at both sizes. The hollow glacier is original, but the row of almost identical prisms reads as fence posts and the three sets of broadcast arcs feel pasted on. At 480, CHOIR OF ICE is clean and comfortably clear of the foreground.

- Establish a foreground soloist and separate grouped reeds into distinct planes.
- Give the sounding apertures throats and replace the repeated arc symbols with a single overlapping acoustic field.

## 173 · Orchard in Transit

The warm cabin makes an immediate focal point against the violet moon, and the bevels and crater polygons are well suited to NAPLPS. The conceit of transporting a living orchard fits the theme and is more distinctive than the capsule silhouette. Four tiny circular crowns and neat pots make it resemble a display case with a decorative tail. ORCHARD IN TRANSIT is clear across the top.

- Show connected planting beds, irrigation, and differentiated branching inside the cabin.
- Make the case into a vessel with segmented window supports and a working drive silhouette.

## 174 · Borrowed Dawn

The focal mirror is clear and the warm shaft against charcoal uses flat NAPLPS fills well. Borrowing a morning for a stranded town is a distinctive story moment. Both beams have heavy, abrupt boundaries, so the direct ray seems to emerge from a small wedge floating on the sun. The stick-braced mount and line of tiny houses feel like props. BORROWED DAWN is fully readable.

- Tie the incoming light visually to the sun and map the reflected beam onto the valley.
- Build a believable hinged array and give the lit settlement streets and terraces.

## 175 · The Truce Line

The teal and rust machines facing across a pale disc make a clear, symmetrical encounter. NAPLPS silhouettes and the mosaic boundary work well as graphic devices. Diplomacy between machine species fits the theme, but the neutral flags stand upright and both claws appear raised for another blow. The empty tan foreground has little to say about the white sea. THE TRUCE LINE is sharp and readable.

- Lower both hands into distinct open gestures and plant each side's banner at a different angle.
- Make the boundary run visibly through a salt bed and ground both machines with contact shadows.
