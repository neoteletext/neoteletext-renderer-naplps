# Set 11 · Improvement round 1

## Critique and priorities

I inspected each original render at 480 × 460 and 640 × 480. The title is the only text on each plate. Every title is readable at 480 × 460, with the individual observations below. The byte counts refer to the original NAPLPS streams.

### 166 · Keelward · 2,177 bytes

The orange and turquoise windows make the inverted towers pop against navy at both sizes, but nine similarly cut towers form a row of teeth, with little depth. Broad filled strata and small rectangular window bars suit NAPLPS. An inverted city under a salt moon is a strong invented setting, though the blank ceiling and dangling hairlines leave the attachment to the crust vague. The ferry could be a fleck of light. KEELWARD reads cleanly in cream at 480, with a slightly crowded W.

- Cut roof pockets and add visible tower roots and a receding skyline.
- Give the foreground a working dock and turn the ferry into a discernible craft.

### 167 · Cinder Mail · 679 bytes

The diagonal is forceful, with cream facets against vermilion and a useful teal accent. The low byte count shows how much filled polygons can do. The sail reads as a familiar kite, however, and the hull is too anonymous to sell a courier working in stellar ash. The uniform bright streaks resemble speed marks, while the darkest panel merges with its surroundings. CINDER MAIL is crisp on the dark upper band at 480.

- Give the sail a leading spar and a clear corner treatment.
- Design a courier hull with a cabin and cargo, and give the ash stream clusters and eddies.

### 168 · Prism Grazer · 924 bytes

The angular torso holds against the flat yellow plain in both renders. Jewel colours and native discs make economical use of solid fills, but the legs look bolted on and the tail appears to end abruptly. The glass animal is a distinctive subject. Its meal reads as a dribble because the light seam and mouth barely interact. PRISM GRAZER stays readable in dark blue at 480.

- Replace the strip of daylight with a fractured luminous seam and show it refracting through the animal.
- Give the legs tapered facets and the tail a crystalline tip.

### 169 · The Second Floor · 917 bytes

The peach and cyan landings make inverted gravity immediately plausible. Nested lines deliver a lot of perspective for the bytes, although the dense grid flattens the space into a wireframe and the surveyors look like generic pictograms. The encounter is a good science-fiction moment. Its action is unclear because both people reach into empty space. THE SECOND FLOOR has strong cream-on-navy contrast and reads in full at 480.

- Build thickness into the landings and make the corridor feel like built space.
- Equip the surveyors and give them an instrument they are both trying to reach.

### 170 · Umbra Loom · 1,079 bytes

The cropped dark disc is striking, and stacked native circles do the work efficiently. Rose filaments have good contrast, but all four bundles collide at one kink, and the shuttles look like identical flat lozenges. The dark-star loom is original. The still barely shows how the machines gather the light. UMBRA LOOM is clear on its quiet lower-right field at 480.

- Draw independent ribbons from different points along the stellar limb and keep the pulse.
- Give the shuttles intake mouths and visible collector hardware.

### 171 · Rainworks · 1,732 bytes

Copper against teal reads well and the native circles and repeating drop macro are economical. The identical vessels and angular brackets at both sizes resemble an ornament border. The drops pack into braids just beyond the nozzles, and there is no sense of what the foundry waters. This makes a specific story about first rain feel like a generic schematic. RAINWORKS is clean and bright at 480.

- Link the pressure vessels into an industrial manifold.
- Space the timed rainfall into distinct drops and give it terrain to hit.

### 172 · Choir of Ice · 1,443 bytes

The dark blues give the pale spires good value contrast, and arc and hatch commands deliver a lot of surface for little data. The brightest hatch almost erases the distinction between the two faces, especially at 640. Spires hang over arbitrary bands and the sound arcs float above them like signal icons. Mineral resonators are a distinctive idea, but the cavern lacks physical depth. CHOIR OF ICE is readable at 480 against the dark footer.

- Seat the reeds in a basin and distinguish rear forms by value.
- Reserve bright hatch for selected faces and show the notes leaving the apertures.

### 173 · Orchard in Transit · 837 bytes

The amber window against a violet moon is clear at both sizes. Native discs make the trees cheaply, yet four copies of the same canopy resemble an icon strip. The moon looks like blank paper with a crease and the capsule has little mechanism to place it in flight. Freight carrying living trees is a specific science-fiction idea. ORCHARD IN TRANSIT reads easily at 480 with plenty of clearance.

- Give the orchard planting beds and individual branching silhouettes.
- Give the capsule propulsion and put visible relief on the moon.

### 174 · Borrowed Dawn · 791 bytes

The two amber beams make an economical vector diagram, with clear contrast at both sizes. The dark terrain floats as a torn strip and the bright slab balances on a crude bent stick. The village is barely more than a row of lit dots. The mirror bringing daylight to a town has a strong premise, but the people making it happen are invisible. BORROWED DAWN is legible on the dark sky at 480.

- Build a grounded mirror gantry and show a worker at its controls.
- Extend the terrain to the foreground and make reflected light visibly land among the buildings.

### 175 · The Truce Line · 731 bytes

The opposed teal and rust bodies stage the confrontation clearly, and custom-width G3 dashes make efficient perspective markers. At both sizes the bodies look like flat animal cutouts, so the machine species feel generic. The washed-out warm palette loses the horizon and the encounter reads as two raised fists. THE TRUCE LINE is readable on cream at 480, although the final E sits close to the plaque edge.

- Differentiate the machines through their frames, joints and lowered hands.
- Give the flags pedestals, make the salt basin more spatial, and widen the title plaque.
