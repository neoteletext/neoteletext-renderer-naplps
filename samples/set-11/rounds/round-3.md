# Round 3 of 4

## Critique and priorities

I inspected each pre-round render at its native 480 × 460 and 640 × 480 dimensions. The title is the only text on each plate. The taller proportions at 480 × 460 make several circles look like ovals and compress fine horizontal details, so the notes distinguish these effects where they matter.

### 166 · Keelward

The warm faces and turquoise windows pop against the gulf, and overlapping filled polygons make the hanging skyline easy to read. The upside-down city is a convincing and original piece of science fiction. But almost all its buildings are the same long striped prism, and the equally spaced horizontal bands below look like a test palette. At 480 the carriage and its supports are particularly easy to miss. **KEELWARD** is fully legible at 480, despite the stroke font bunching the middle of the W. Priorities are to give the main tower a recognizable civic structure and turn the empty gulf bands into depth cues for the ferry.

### 167 · Cinder Mail

The pale sail is a strong diagonal, and the warm current makes the teal hull conspicuous. Native polygon fills suit the taut panels, but the biggest cream area is blank and the individual tide bands look mechanically parallel. Several ash marks look like isolated short underlines. A sailboat in stellar ash is a good genre fit, though the courier itself is generic. **CINDER MAIL** is fully legible at 480. Priorities are to show how the fabric is assembled and make the current visibly deflect around the sail, with a larger, distinct courier cabin.

### 168 · Prism Grazer

The lowered head and split light make feeding a readable action. The acid ground and navy contour separate the body nicely, and the polygon facets exploit the medium. The animal reads as a generic angular quadruped until the optics register. The straight middle-distance bands feel unfinished, and at 480 the white ray makes the cramped muzzle look like a tangle. **PRISM GRAZER** is fully legible at 480. Priorities are to give the head a clear light-collecting anatomy and add a physical trail of refracted light across broken terrain.

### 169 · The Second Floor

Apricot and teal establish the two orientations, and the native polygon perspective does most of the storytelling. The instrument exchange is a specific, original use of an otherwise familiar gravity reversal. The corridor is close to a wireframe exercise. Its lines meet directly behind the instrument and compete with the event. One hanging arm on each suit ends bluntly. **THE SECOND FLOOR** is fully legible at 480, with ample contrast in its dark plaque. Priorities are to frame the handoff with an engineered transition and give the figures visibly planted boots and finished suit hardware.

### 170 · Umbra Loom

The cropped stellar rim and diagonal shuttles create an unusually strong silhouette. The native circles, crossing lines and blinking colour make a distinctive image of an invented trade. The broad circles look too evenly spaced to be active stellar material, and four nearly identical purple wedges read as repeated icons. The lower space is especially bare at 640. **UMBRA LOOM** is fully legible at 480. Priorities are to break the limb with layered prominences and show how the shuttles feed captured light into storage.

### 171 · Rainworks

The coral copper against green makes a clear industrial frieze. Circles, repeated drop macros and WAIT are apt for machinery discharging in unison. Artificial rain is an original subject, but the disconnected spheres look like gauges hung from brackets, and the large central discharge looks like a solid cone. **RAINWORKS** is fully legible at 480. Priorities are to show the feed manifold and the mechanical banding of the vessels, then give the central jet a hollow, breaking spray and give the basin a sense of moving water.

### 172 · Choir of Ice

The tall foreground reed and warm arcs stand out in cobalt. Hatch is a good shorthand for shaded mineral faces. The sounding glacier fits the invented-world theme and feels less familiar than the usual crystal cave. However, the reeds look mass-produced, the back wall is broad and flat, and a few arcs start visibly in midair. **CHOIR OF ICE** is fully legible at 480, even with the central reed pointing toward it. Priorities are to root the arcs at active apertures and make the reeds and surrounding cave read as fractured ice.

### 173 · Orchard in Transit

The bright planted bay is an effective warm window into a cold scene, with filled polygons dividing hull and cargo cleanly. Shipping an orchard is a distinctive bit of worldbuilding. The round crowns look like repeated lollipops, the dark rear of the hull nearly disappears at 480, and the engine sparks are too slight to suggest a loaded vessel in motion. **ORCHARD IN TRANSIT** is fully legible at 480. Priorities are to make the living cargo varied and visibly tended, and give the freighter recognizable propulsion and cargo structure.

### 174 · Borrowed Dawn

The mirror and two warm beams form a strong bent path through the dark sky. The hard polygon edges suit the directed light. Supplying a town with morning is a memorable premise, though the buildings look like a row of icons, with almost all the illumination inside the windows. The oversized concentric halo reads like a separate target and competes with the reflector. **BORROWED DAWN** is fully legible at 480. Priorities are to clarify the light's source and its relationship to the reflector, and to make the beam produce readable illumination and cast shadows across a terraced town.

### 175 · The Truce Line

The warm ground frames two distinct machine silhouettes and a strikingly quiet negotiation scene. The mosaic dashes make good economical use of the medium to mark perspective. The open manipulators are less clear at 480, and the plain circular backdrop and flat shelves make the white sea look like an empty desert. The premise feels original, especially where the two machines differ in build. **THE TRUCE LINE** is fully legible at 480. Priorities are to add water and seabed cues, and to make the two hands and the shared truce gesture more explicit.

## Changes made

- **166 · Keelward.** Built a ring-shaped transfer hall with two occupied galleries and a lift on the central tower. Replaced the straight gulf bands with overlapping salt banks and gave the ferry a brighter, split passage through them.
- **167 · Cinder Mail.** Added cross-seams and reinforcing tape to the sail, traced the ash flow around its leading and trailing edges, and enlarged the courier's glazed cabin.
- **168 · Prism Grazer.** Sculpted a pronged intake and separated the eye from the feeding ray. Added refracted patches across the near fracture and scattered exposed stone on the plain.
- **169 · The Second Floor.** Placed a split-colour gravity collar behind the handoff, added recessed wall panels, and finished the surveyors' boots, gloves and suit controls.
- **170 · Umbra Loom.** Broke up the dark star's limb with offset bands and local prominences. Gave the shuttles exposed storage cells and conduits that lead back to the ribbon guides.
- **171 · Rainworks.** Connected the pressure vessels to a manifold, banded the tanks with bolted flanges, and replaced the solid central discharge with separate spray streams feeding ripples in the basin.
- **172 · Choir of Ice.** Added broken shelves and mineral fractures. Rebuilt the three coloured wave groups so every arc starts at an active aperture and fits inside both render sizes.
- **173 · Orchard in Transit.** Gave the trees angular foliage fans of different heights, added emitters along the planting bed, and made the hull ribs, lower housings and twin exhaust wakes more visible.
- **174 · Borrowed Dawn.** Replaced the broad halo with a small crescent behind the light source and joined the incoming beam to the source. Added lit terrace edges, bright roof faces and directional building shadows.
- **175 · The Truce Line.** Replaced the circular backdrop with light through the white water, added seabed ripples, and articulated the two open grippers beside the planted flags.
