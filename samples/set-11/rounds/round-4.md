# Round 4 of 4

## Critique and priorities

Reviewed the incoming PNGs at native 480 × 460 and 640 × 480. The title is the only text on each plate.

### 166 · Keelward

The crowded band of coral towers makes a good silhouette against the blue gulf, but repeated stripes flatten the skyline and the transfer hall is overpowered by the buildings behind it. The hanging salt-moon city is an unusual setting. Native polygons and wide strokes describe its architecture well. At 640 the ferry reads as a flattened arrow. At 480 its size and blue surroundings almost erase the hull. **KEELWARD** reads cleanly at 480. Priorities are to give the main tower a visibly load-bearing foundation and make the gulf foreground tell the story of the ferry crossing.

### 167 · Cinder Mail

The cream sail cuts across red ash and sets up a strong diagonal against the teal cockpit. Polygon fills and swept lines suit the sail, but the bands run edge to edge like coloured paper, and the tight lower triangle of stays reads as another solid panel. At 480 the tiny cabin is still identifiable. At 640 there is room to see how little the hull explains the courier's job. The ash courier is a good genre idea, with a familiar sailboat silhouette. **CINDER MAIL** reads cleanly at 480. Priorities are to build a real cargo and rigging assembly and give the current distinct layers of turbulent debris.

### 168 · Prism Grazer

The navy silhouette makes the angular animal unmistakable on acid yellow, and vector facets make a glass metabolism plausible. Feeding on exposed light is one of the more original concepts. The flat yellow upper field and skinny mechanical limbs make the setting look like an emblem. At 480 the forked snout nearly merges into a white knot, and at 640 the internal light paths compete with the contour. **PRISM GRAZER** reads cleanly at 480. Priorities are to give the animal a distinctive light-collecting anatomy and simplify the bright contact point while showing more of the structure of the seam.

### 169 · The Second Floor

Recursion in the teal and apricot frames leads directly to the handoff, a specific and original incident in a familiar gravity-reversal setting. NAPLPS straight lines and flat fills suit the corridor. The platforms look disconnected from its structure, and the matching figures are oddly stiff. At 480 the instrument's two thin handles blend into the fingers. At 640 the vast side walls look largely unbuilt. **THE SECOND FLOOR** reads in full at 480. Priorities are to make the two decks look anchored and the exchange unmistakably deliberate through a larger instrument and clearer grips.

### 170 · Umbra Loom

The aubergine disc and bright pink tangents have excellent diagonal flow. Native curves and palette animation are particularly well suited to this invented industry. The repeated pointed purple shuttles look like copies of a single cursor, and the low-contrast outer bands are empty at 640. At 480 the hairline intersections fuse into a little scribble. **UMBRA LOOM** remains clear at 480. Priorities are to establish a different scale and role for the lead shuttle and turn the filaments into broad translucent-looking ribbons with a readable pulse.

### 171 · Rainworks

Copper orbs against deep green make for good colour separation, but the straight elevation and same-height vessels make the foundry look like a plumbing diagram. WAIT and repeated macro drops make the fiction of a first rain release credible. The central stream reads as a bent wire cage at 640 and as a mess of lines at 480. **RAINWORKS** is fully legible at 480. Priorities are to make the central release a sheet of water and show where the water is gathered and conveyed.

### 172 · Choir of Ice

The blue architecture and coral trajectories are striking, and native hatch and arcs separate shadow from sound. The pitch-producing glacier is an original invented-world conceit. The foreground reed looks like a clean rectangular column with a token fracture, while identical pink trails resemble thrown objects. Hatch is busier at 480, and at 640 the empty mass of the largest face is obvious. **CHOIR OF ICE** remains clear at 480. Priorities are to give the soloist a visibly broken, layered ice face and use intersecting wavefronts to imply resonance.

### 173 · Orchard in Transit

The amber interior is a strong focal point against violet and the angular hull is well matched to polygon fills. Moving an orchard through space has an original human purpose, although the cargo looks like four houseplants in an oversized window. At 480 the thrusters are squeezed into the left edge. At 640 the moon reads as a purple disk with several cut-out holes. **ORCHARD IN TRANSIT** reads in full at 480. Priorities are to give the cargo a sense of human scale and agricultural function and to turn the moon into an ice landscape with depth.

### 174 · Borrowed Dawn

The warm angular beams direct the eye from sky to mirror to roofs, and native fills make the boundary of the light unusually precise. The premise of supplying morning to a town is memorable. The figure and houses still look like separate symbols, while the dark lower quarter and much of the right-hand sky are empty. At 480 several tiny houses merge into a single dark strip. **BORROWED DAWN** reads at 480. Priorities are to make the machine visibly steer the mirror and use more of the valley to show what the dawn actually changes.

### 175 · The Truce Line

Teal and rust silhouettes balance well on the pale seabed, and the widening G3 dashes draw the eye toward the flags. Two machine cultures negotiating underwater is distinctive. The flags are the same shape, and the bent arms look tangled at 480. At 640 the hard light shaft and continuous ground read more like a dry desert. **THE TRUCE LINE** is clear at 480. Priorities are to give the negotiators a specific shared gesture around the boundary and place unmistakable submerged structures behind them.
