# Ten text pages / 111–120

[640 × 480 contact sheet](out/contact-sheet.png) · [512 × 400 contact sheet](out/contact-sheet-512x400.png) · [Manifest](out/manifest.json) · [Render validation](out/validation.json) · [Stream coverage audit](out/coverage-audit.json)

These ten fictional pages demonstrate the sixteen facilities in BRIEF.md through useful printed and terminal forms. Every final page was rendered and visually inspected individually at **640 × 480 and 512 × 400**. The contact sheets have five columns and two rows. All letters use the supplied **microstar.strokes** font; the cinema's explicitly defined star is the only DRCS glyph. Mosaics are native characters. There are no raster pictures in any NAP stream.

Every stream begins exactly `0E 20 7F 7F`, explicitly restores the character sets, defines **all sixteen colour-map entries**, uses only 7-bit bytes, and ends with END and SO. Files range from **588 to 1,072 bytes**, below even a decimal 6 KB. Nine pages are static. Only 119 blinks; no page uses WAIT, and no information depends on animation. Its normal PNG uses `--tenths 5`; additional images at 0, 7, 12 and 16 tenths show the other ornament states. Automated image differences confirm that those states change only the small ornament area.

The guide, reference, terminal text implementation and stroke compositor were read before design. These are checks against the supplied renderer, **not PP3 captures**. The source's acknowledged differences in large and rotated stroke placement remain relevant to comparison with PP3. No renderer or other file outside this working directory was changed.

## Page notes

**111 — Mere Type Bureau · 795 bytes · covers 1, 2, 3.** [640](out/111-mere-type.png) · [512](out/111-mere-type-512x400.png) · [NAP](out/111-mere-type.nap). An ivory printer's proof uses a vermilion `Aa` above two columns of real specimen copy. The five labelled samples are sent with **SMALL `1B 4A`, MEDIUM `1B 4B`, NORMAL `1B 4C`, DOUBLE HEIGHT `1B 4D`, and DOUBLE SIZE `1B 4F`**, rather than approximated with TEXT. The hero's TEXT field is **104 × 120 pixels**, exactly a quarter of the 480-pixel screen high. The order line uses an **8 × 12** field. Proportional display text and fixed preset specimens show different typographic voices without crowding the sheet.

**112 — The Little Journey · 781 bytes · covers 2, 9, 10.** [640](out/112-pocket-phrases.png) · [512](out/112-pocket-phrases-512x400.png) · [NAP](out/112-pocket-phrases.nap). A navy pocket travel card pairs three large quoted phrases with English translations. French supplies grave and acute accents and a cedilla in its language label; German supplies umlauts and supplementary `ß`; Spanish supplies the non-spacing tilde. Accent bytes **precede their base letters**. Supplementary guillemets enclose the phrases, left and right arrows guide the listening/speaking footer, and **£, ¥ and ¢** form the small-change reminder. The accents are fixed pitch in colour mode 1 to preserve their placement and avoid observation 1 below. Exact POINT SET placements keep language bands aligned.

**113 — The Copper Spoon · 851 bytes · covers 8, 9, 11, 12.** [640](out/113-copper-spoon.png) · [512](out/113-copper-spoon-512x400.png) · [NAP](out/113-copper-spoon.nap). A forest-green supper menu gives soup, pie, salad and tart believable prices. Each dot leader is one period followed by **REPEAT `1B 46 4C`**, adding twelve periods. A narrow active FIELD confines the top underscore **REPEAT TO EOL `1B 47`** rule; another confines a repeated-space fill in colour mode 2 near the foot. The section label is underlined with `1B 59`/`1B 5A`. **REVERSE VIDEO `1B 48`/`1B 49`** exchanges foreground and background for the bread-included badge. Supplementary pounds and the **½ and ¼** portion symbols carry actual menu information. All mode-2 text is fixed pitch, avoiding both observed compositor problems.

**114 — North Quay Radio · 957 bytes · covers 4, 8, 12.** [640](out/114-quay-radio.png) · [512](out/114-quay-radio-512x400.png) · [NAP](out/114-quay-radio.nap). A harbour watch terminal shows the final eight received lines, from 18:36 to 18:42. The stream actually sends earlier records, enables **SCROLL ON `1B 57`**, and scrolls those records out of an active **562.5 × 240** FIELD while the title, clock and outer frame remain fixed. **CR/LF** returns to this field's left edge and advances by **3/2** of its 20-pixel character height. The 18:41 message is padded to exactly forty cells; `QUAY / WELCOME HOME` follows without a line break, demonstrating character-edge wrapping inside the field. This uses no word-wrap facility. SCROLL OFF and FIELD with no operands restore normal behaviour. Mode 2 clears newly exposed rows to the panel background. The file records the stream offset of this demonstration in its evidence JSON.

**115 — Turning Leaves · 682 bytes · covers 5, 6.** [640](out/115-turning-leaves.png) · [512](out/115-turning-leaves-512x400.png) · [NAP](out/115-turning-leaves.nap). Two cloth spines stand beside a reversible novel in a library display. `THE LONG WAY` combines **rotation 90° with path up** (`TEXT` first byte `49`), while `A RIVER BELOW` combines **270° with path down** (`4F`). `BEFORE THE RAIN` reads normally on the ochre cover; `AFTER THE FLOOD` combines **180° with path left** (`46`) and invites the reader to turn the book. Ordinary horizontal headings supply rotation 0° and path right. Thus all four paths and rotations are visible in a single coherent display, and each nonzero rotation is combined with the appropriate path rather than simulated with individually positioned letters.

**116 — The Quiet Between · 688 bytes · covers 3, 4, 12.** [640](out/116-quiet-between.png) · [512](out/116-quiet-between-512x400.png) · [NAP](out/116-quiet-between.nap). Four brief stanzas sit between a small ochre moon and a distant pale shoreline. Stanzas I–IV use **character spacing 1, 5/4, 3/2 and proportional**, respectively, together with **row spacing 1, 5/4, 3/2 and 2**. Each stanza is one text run using actual CR/LF inside its own active FIELD, so the whitespace follows TEXT's row-spacing bits rather than manually placed lines. Fixed-width `A sail / unthreads / the blue` opens out across the lower left; proportional `The tide returns` closes into an intimate lower-right voice. The poem and author are invented. Its proportional text is colour mode 1.

**117 — Pen & Point · 1,072 bytes · covers 7, 10.** [640](out/117-pen-and-point.png) · [512](out/117-pen-and-point-512x400.png) · [NAP](out/117-pen-and-point.nap). A technical workshop card repeats the same operation in four panels: position at A, type `INK`, move the drawing point to B with POINT SET, type `X`, and draw one absolute LINE. TEXT second bytes **40, 44, 48 and 4C** select **together, cursor leads, point leads and independent**. Together moves the X to B and starts the line after it; cursor leads leaves X after INK and starts the line after X; point leads puts X at B but starts the line at B; independent leaves X after INK while the line starts at B. The amber A/B marks are drawn before the demonstration. The ivory diagonals are the actual resulting pen strokes. No cursor display is involved, and no corrective placement fakes the differences.

**118 — Isle Air Boarding Card · 678 bytes · covers 10.** [640](out/118-isle-air.png) · [512](out/118-isle-air-512x400.png) · [NAP](out/118-isle-air.nap). A cream ticket gives invented passenger Lena Moss a flight from Merehaven to Oriel. **APH `1E`** positions the airline heading at home. **NSR `1F 42 42`** places the flight at top-origin row 2, column 2 and resets the text/domain state while preserving the palette. The generator consequently restores its four-byte DOMAIN before later geometry. **APS `1C 2E 22`** places the passenger at renderer row 14, column 2. In the lower active field, **HT `09` is one character forward**, not a tab-stop operation: it separates GATE and A4. **LF `0A`** retains x for the boarding time, **VT `0B`** returns upward for the seat, and **CR `0D` plus LF** returns to the field margin for BOARD and DEPART. POINT SET handles the precise route and stamp placements. APS's unverified row convention is called out below.

**119 — The Glass Horizon · 588 bytes · covers 11, 13, 14, 15.** [640](out/119-glass-horizon.png) · [512](out/119-glass-horizon-512x400.png) · [NAP](out/119-glass-horizon.nap). An apricot and lilac cinema notice promotes a fictional Friday film. A full-block **mosaic `SS3 7F`**, underline mode and REPEAT produce the separated marquee bar; supplementary-looking bars in the admission line are actually **mosaic `SS3 23` characters mixed with primary text**. A custom five-point star is defined with **DEF DRCS `1B 43 21`**, ended, designated into G2 with `1B 2A 7B`, then invoked as **`19 21` in the ordinary line `A [star] SABLE FILM / COLOUR`**. This permanent logo uses entry 1. Only two small lower-right ornaments animate: a primary asterisk in dedicated entry 14 uses **BLINK START `1B 4E`**, and a primary plus sign in entry 15 uses **PDI BLINK `3F 74 47 49 40`**, alternating with amber entry 13 for 7/9 tenths. The film title, time, price and logo are permanent. No WAIT is sent.

**120 — The Ribbon Check · 795 bytes · covers 2, 10, 16.** [640](out/120-ribbon-check.png) · [512](out/120-ribbon-check-512x400.png) · [NAP](out/120-ribbon-check.nap). A slightly offset cream typewriter service slip sits on a grey desk. Its short letter uses genuine CR/LF, HT and **three BS `08` controls** to return over `red`, overstrike it with `///`, and continue with `black`; the cancellation is visible at both resolutions. The lower keyboard impression sends **every printable primary character, 20–7E**, in three native text runs: 20–3F, 40–5F and 60–7E. That includes the space, both cases, all digits, and commonly missed backslash, backtick, braces, vertical bar, caret and tilde. The stream audit follows shifts and excludes operands, so these are verified as primary text rather than merely bytes present somewhere in the drawing. Small workshop annotations contrast with the larger 16 × 25 impression fields.

## Renderer observations

The following two behaviours appear wrong relative to the repository reference's text model. They are reproducible observations against this renderer; no claim of a PP3 comparison is made. The complete probe files contain RESET, sixteen palette definitions and the stated DOMAIN. [Probe generator](probe_renderer.py) · [Comparison image](out/probes/comparison.png) · [Exact pixel comparisons](out/probes/results.json).

**1. Colour mode 2 erases a non-spacing accent when its base letter arrives.** The reference says supplementary 40–4F are non-spacing accents and explicitly gives `19 42 65` for é. With four-byte coordinates (`21 4C ...`), the reproducing tail is **`22 40 40 40 5C 49 6B 3E 44 40 24 41 6D 44 43 0F 19 42 65 0E`**: a fixed 64 × 80 field, foreground 1/background 0, POINT SET, then acute accent and e. Expected: the composed accented letter, just as in mode 1. Observed: plain e. The images from [accented mode 2](out/probes/fixed-acute-mode2.nap) and [plain e mode 2](out/probes/fixed-e-mode2.nap) are **pixel-identical at both resolutions**. In mode 1, adding the accent changes 209 pixels at 640 and 149 at 512. In `terminal.rs::character` each character receives the mode-2 background, and `text.rs::draw` paints the base field over the accent drawn moments earlier. **Design workaround:** all multilingual accents are drawn in colour mode 1; mode-2 badges and log text have no accents.

**2. Proportional mode-2 background fields erase preceding letters.** With the same DOMAIN, position and field size, the reproducing tail is **`22 70 40 40 5C 49 6B 3E 44 40 24 41 6D 44 43 0F 69 6C 6C 0E`**. `70` requests proportional rightward text; the primary text is `ill`. Expected: three narrowly spaced letters, as in mode 1, against the background. Observed: only the last l survives. [The `ill` probe](out/probes/prop-ill-mode2.nap) is **pixel-identical at both sizes** to [one l positioned at x=132](out/probes/prop-last-l-mode2.nap); mode 1 correctly retains all three letters. The mode-1/mode-2 difference is 707 pixels at 640 and 510 at 512. The proportional left-bearing shift moves each full background field behind its cursor, over ink from preceding characters. **Design workaround:** mode-2 and reverse badges are fixed pitch; proportional titles and poetry use mode 1 on a previously painted ground.

**Compatibility observations, without a defect claim.** APS currently counts rows upward from the display bottom: `1C 2E 22` lands at x=32 with baseline y=144 in the 640 render. NSR's optional position counts downward from the top: `1F 42 42` lands at x=32, baseline y=72. The reference explicitly says APS has not been checked against PP3, so 118 intentionally uses the implemented convention; the manifest does not imply that convention is standards-verified. FIELD containment also depends on the *encoded* character dimensions. A nominal 14-pixel width rounds to 14.0625 with four-byte operands; forty cells therefore need 562.5 pixels, not 560. Page 114 uses that exact field width and a small vertical inset. This is coordinate quantisation, not reported as a renderer defect. Separated mosaics use the documented tenth-field gap, and large/rotated strokes retain the documented PP3 placement/thickness differences. None of the pages depends on word wrap, a drawn cursor, or input-field behaviour.

## Reproduction and checks

Run `python3 build.py` to regenerate all ten streams, render both requested sizes, verify deterministic regeneration, enforce sizes/7-bit/RESET, produce the manifest and 5 × 2 contact sheets, and inspect the cinema's alternate blink phases by pixel bounds. Run `python3 audit.py` for an independent byte inventory and the coverage checks. Run `python3 probe_renderer.py` to reproduce the two issues. The encoder and individual page generators use only Python's standard library. Preview assembly and pixel comparisons use installed Pillow; rendering uses the supplied release `naplps-render`, Microstar stroke file and ffmpeg. `out/validation.json` records SHA-256 hashes, sizes and colour counts; `out/coverage-audit.json` records actual controls, TEXT bitfields, character sets and PDI offsets. The visual review found no clipped copy or unintended overlaps after the final changes; the typewriter overstrike is intentional.
