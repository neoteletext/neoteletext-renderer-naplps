# Round 3 · Typographic refinement

## Review of the incoming renders

Both native renders were inspected for each page. The text inventories below describe the 480 × 460 read.

### 156 · Silt

The rust section and cream knockouts give SILT the authority of an exhibition poster, with unusually apt wedge-cut DRCS terminals. “Sediment in relief”, “BED 01”, “SILT”, “Exhibition in layers” and “BED 02” all read. The I sits in a conspicuously wide gap after S. The pale lower band makes the thin fault look like a scratch, especially at 640, and the upper and lower bed labels feel orphaned. The cutaway is original but its bottom edge is too schematic for the subject.

**Priorities.** Fit the four capitals by their visible edges. Make the fault visibly displace the laminae. Attach the bed numbers to the section with short leaders.

### 157 · Quarter Turn

The rotated native lettering is the poster: both QUARTER and both TURN readings are clear when followed in their intended orientation, and 90° reads cleanly. Orchid and lime have sharp contrast on black. The 480 dial becomes a tall ellipse and its muted ticks nearly disappear. The wide corner brackets have the visual weight of a crop overlay and the triangular arrow looks stuck onto the scale. The reading circuit is inventive enough to carry the whole composition.

**Priorities.** Rebuild the dial hierarchy with midpoint ticks and a clearer selected quadrant. Integrate the arc head into the sweep and trim the corner furniture.

### 158 · The Quireline

“The Quireline”, “Issue 04”, “COVER STORY”, “Inside the”, “paper fold”, “A journal of” and “folded matter” all read. The issue number nearly competes with the masthead at channel size. Pink and ivory carry the grid well, but the broad blunt Q tail and repeated parallel edges resemble a logo dropped on a book icon. The transparent DRCS counter revealing the fold is a distinctly typographic use of the medium. At 640 the three right-hand blocks have almost identical widths and little editorial hierarchy.

**Priorities.** Draw a more contrasted Q and taper its tail. Refine the folded planes and leaf edges. Give the cover line and issue line separate, deliberate roles.

### 159 · Ink and Air

INK, AIR, both pairs of 1 and 2, “Stroke”, “Counters” and the display B are clear. The mint reversal is strong and the reuse of the exact DRCS contours is rigorous. The right field feels empty because the counters float without a visible parent. Guides continue across the B and slice through the very stroke the image is meant to explain. At 640 that interference is even more obvious. The reciprocal specimen is one of the most original concepts in the set.

**Priorities.** Show a light parent outline behind the isolated counters. Move measurement marks away from the printed stroke. Use the captions to identify the positive and negative forms precisely.

### 160 · Low Water

“Low water”, “The tide takes”, “its blue back”, “one syllable” and “at a time.” all read. The teal reversal and delayed last phrase suit the poem. Native proportional text does the work of pacing, though the first three lines stop almost at the same right edge and the final line looks detached rather than submerged. The angular foam traces look like unrelated lightning bolts at both sizes. The original concrete poem fits the brief closely.

**Priorities.** Pace the lines by their approach to the shore and explicitly set off the last phrase in the water. Draw receding tidal contours from the shore geometry itself.

### 161 · Register 258

“Register”, “Tabular”, 258, 825 and “Stencilled figures” are legible. Amber and slate make a convincing instrument face. The matched DRCS cuts are distinctive, but the minor row shows a smaller scale rather than visibly demonstrating equal advance. The blue lines show through the cuts and make the stencil openings look like bars, more obviously at 640. The type specimen has strong raw material and weak proof.

**Priorities.** Show two reordered rows at equal scale. Move the column center markers out of the numeral bodies and use full-width rails to tie the cells together.

### 162 · Oblique Assembly

“Oblique Assembly”, LEAN, “Drawing”, “italic capitals”, “30 run” and “100 rise” read. Black on lemon is decisive and the shared shear of guides and polygons is well suited to NAPLPS. The A looks pinched on its right leg, particularly at 640, and the isolated triangle is only loosely related to the full-size drawing. The grid and word are a strong workshop poster, though the construction is currently more decorative than explanatory.

**Priorities.** Rebalance the A and its counter. Extend the construction with an upright reference and measure the same shear with anchored dimension lines.

### 163 · Three Voices

“Three Voices”, all three VOX settings and “For unaccompanied voices” read, though the footer is crowded at 480. Cream, pink and violet separate the entries without sacrificing contrast. Proportional DRCS scaling is effective, but the faint dotted lines vanish at channel size and the three words read as a descending size chart before they read as a score. The word choices and composition are original, with room for a more convincing temporal structure.

**Priorities.** Give each entry a clear start and duration. Anchor all three to a legible shared beat ruler. Open up the programme line.

### 164 · The Fi Join

“The fi join”, “Hook”, “Join”, “fit” and “One stroke, two letters” read at 480. Ivory and vermilion make an effective drawing plate. The large DRCS ligature reads, although the short spacing around the dot makes the hook and i feel crowded. The Join leader lands on the i stem after crossing the bar, and the small “fit” is too isolated to explain the join. At 640 the callout line crossing the red form is especially raw. A custom ligature is exactly right for this set.

**Priorities.** Give the dot more air and point the leaders to real features. Show an unjoined f and i alongside the joined form at the same scale.

### 165 · A Room After Dark

“A room after dark”, OPEN, LATE and “Read until dawn” all read. Cobalt and apricot suggest a sign at night, and palette animation belongs here. The lettering has the silhouette of solid extrusion, with thick joints swallowing the inner light and brittle little end offsets at 640. The rows and square lamps look mechanically identical. The original sign merits a more convincing luminous tube treatment.

**Priorities.** Draw continuous light cores with rounded bends and warm halos. Build the divider and perimeter lamps into one sign rhythm.

## Changes made

- **156 · Silt** — Refitted the visible edges of the four DRCS capitals, displaced the lower laminae across a filled fault, and connected both bed labels to their layers.
- **157 · Quarter Turn** — Raised dial contrast, graded the subdivision ticks, integrated the arrow into the highlighted sweep, and shortened the corner brackets.
- **158 · The Quireline** — Cut a thinner Q counter wall and a tapered tail, added a narrow fold plane, and gave the masthead, issue line, cover story and journal description separate visual weights.
- **159 · Ink and Air** — Reused the B contour as an outline on the reverse panel, moved both sets of numbered guides into the margins, and labelled the specimens “Printed B” and “Counterforms”.
- **160 · Low Water** — Staggered the verse and set the last phrase in open water between five tapered traces derived from the shoreline.
- **161 · Register 258** — Set 258 and 825 at the same scale on the same column centers. Moved the center marks to full-width rails, clearing the stencil cuts.
- **162 · Oblique Assembly** — Broadened the A’s right leg, recut the counter, and added an upright and dimension marks to the full-size drawing and the shear diagram.
- **163 · Three Voices** — Set the three VOX entries against an evenly spaced beat ruler, extended their duration rules to a shared release, and shortened the programme line.
- **164 · The Fi Join** — Moved the i-dot away from the hook, kept the callouts outside the display shape, and drew separate DRCS f and i forms for a same-scale comparison to the ligature.
- **165 · A Room After Dark** — Drew concentric tube strokes with filled rounded joints and warm halos. Aligned the divider with the middle perimeter lamps and gave the lamps a circular profile.

## Verification

Regenerated ten streams, twenty native previews, the contact sheet and the manifest. All ten passed `validate.py`, including deterministic generation, 7-bit encoding, full reset, all sixteen colour-map definitions and clean renderer diagnostics. Reviewed the revised renders at both sizes. The two marquee phases differ only in lamp colour at both resolutions. The stream byte ceiling had already been removed, and the current generator and validator audit found only informational stream byte counts.
