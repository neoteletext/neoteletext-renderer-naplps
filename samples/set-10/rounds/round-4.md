# Round 4 · Typography

## Before editing

I compared the 480 × 460 channel render and the 640 × 480 render for every page. The channel format compresses horizontal spacing and changes the apparent weight of the display forms.

### 156 · Silt

The cream headline reads crisply on rust, and the fault through the thin beds is the most convincing bit of geology. The huge central bed is otherwise a flat slab, with abrupt wedges at its edges and a near-tangent where the top bed meets the T. The DRCS cuts make the title specific to the poster, though the I feels railroad-heavy next to the open L. Blending a cutaway and a display face is a distinctive fit for the brief. At 480, SILT, “Sediment in relief,” “Exhibition in layers,” and both BED labels read. The BED 01 leader has a long, empty drop and the BED 02 leader nearly touches the footer rule.

Priorities: Build a finer, continuous set of beds and give the title a clear band of its own. Refit the display capitals and land both leaders decisively on the geology.

### 157 · Quarter Turn

Lime and orchid make the reading circuit immediately apparent. The four genuine rotations are the page's strongest idea, and the dial is a natural typographic reason for them. The circular 640 version is more balanced than the tall channel oval. The isolated corner marks supply little structure, and the oversized arrowhead appears to dent the dial. Both QUARTERs, both TURNs and 90° read at 480 when the corresponding edge is treated as the baseline. The upside-down setting necessarily takes longer to read. The instrument motif is familiar, but repeating the title as a four-way reading is original.

Priorities: Make the sweep a distinct track with clean endpoints and an arrow that follows the curve. Replace the detached corner marks with a frame that identifies four physical sides.

### 158 · The Quireline

Pink against ink violet is a credible cover palette, and the two-column split holds at both sizes. The masthead's thin rhythm is undermined by the dense, over-dark cover headline, while the Q's diamond-like spur reads as a gash through the counter. The leaf edges look like evenly duplicated outlines. The knockout in the DRCS initial gives the folded-paper concept an original, medium-specific hook. At 480 “The Quireline,” “Issue 04,” “COVER STORY,” “Inside the,” “paper fold,” and both lines of “A journal of folded matter” all read, though the headline has congested strokes and the Q tail dominates the column.

Priorities: Redraw the initial as a controlled high-contrast Q and give the folded stack believable edges. Rebalance the masthead furniture and lighten the story setting.

### 159 · Ink and Air

The reversed mint fields make a rigorous specimen, and showing the same two counters as holes and as solid shapes is an original explanation of DRCS masking. At 640 the outer B outline on the right looks polygonal next to the filled left version. At 480 INK, AIR, Printed B, Counterforms, both 1s and both 2s read, but Counterforms is noticeably cramped. The short numbered ticks look detached from the shapes they are supposed to compare.

Priorities: Use the same smoother outline in both panels and make the paired measurement marks actually register with the counter geometry. Give the lower captions equal typographic weight.

### 160 · Low Water

Muted blue-green and pale shore give the little poem breathing room. All five readings are clear at 480: “Low water,” “The tide takes,” “its blue back,” “one syllable,” and “at a time.” The reversed final phrase and proportional, progressively shorter lines make the composition a convincing concrete poem. The striations are mostly long straight segments, and their abrupt starts compete with the last phrase. The title underline feels like furniture from another page. The idea of the words losing ground to water is more original than the present zigzag shoreline.

Priorities: Draw a more fluid shoreline and coherent traces that leave a clean pocket around the reversal. Make the first three verse lines recede by a deliberate typographic rhythm, with the title given its own space.

### 161 · Register 258

Amber on deep navy is sharp and apt for a mechanical specimen. At 480 “Register,” “Tabular,” “Equal advance,” both 258 and 825 read. The common-width DRCS numerals are the design, but the equal-sized rows compete for attention and the ticks give no evidence of actual sidebearings. The rigid box matrix is familiar instrument styling. The original stencil cuts give it character, although the low cuts in 2 and 5 look like chips taken from the baseline.

Priorities: Give 258 the display role and use a smaller second permutation to show the same center axes. Draw actual cell boundaries and revise the stencil cuts as deliberate diagonals.

### 162 · Oblique Assembly

Acid yellow, black and blue are vivid at both sizes. LEAN, “Oblique Assembly,” “Drawing,” “italic capitals,” “30 run” and “100 rise” read at 480. LEAN makes a strong original workshop title, and the native geometry makes the shear demonstrable. The construction graphic over N puts a dotted upright directly beside a thick stem, and the cramped lower triangle looks like a stray sliver. The angular A has too little clear space beside N.

Priorities: Refine LEAN's fit and construction lines. Show the upright and its sheared counterpart at matching scale in a separate diagram with room for the measurements.

### 163 · Three Voices

Cream, pink and lilac are all visible over burgundy, and scaling one DRCS word gives the three entries the same voiceprint. “Three Voices,” all three VOXs, and “Unaccompanied voices” read at 480. The display letters are well formed for a score poster, but grid strokes show through the counters by accident as much as by intent. Each ending is marked separately even though all three entries end together. The staggered score fits the theme and is more distinctive than a simple repetition poster.

Priorities: Give the three voices one shared release marker and distinguish the score's time grid from the type. Arrange the entries so their delays and durations have a clear reading order.

### 164 · The Fi Join

Vermilion on ivory is strong, and the handmade hooked DRCS drawing is one of the most distinctive letters in the set. The title, Hook, Join, footer, giant fi, small f and i, arrow-like greater-than sign and final fi all resolve at 480. The miniature specimens are crowded and make the greater-than sign look like punctuation. The hook leader nearly merges with the ligature, and the join leader points to the outside contour instead of the connection. The comparison is specific to type design and worth making more exact.

Priorities: Derive the separate letters and ligature from shared contours so the join is the variable. Rebuild the comparison with a real directional arrow and point the leaders at exact features.

### 165 · A Room After Dark

Peach tubes stand out beautifully against cobalt. The card's proportions are steady across both formats, although OPEN looks broader than LATE in substance even when their outside edges align. “A room after dark,” OPEN, LATE and “Read until dawn” are all clear at 480. The glowing layered vectors and palette animation are good native uses, while the chamfer and bulb array borrow the familiar shape of a commercial sign. The face behind the type looks flat, and the A crossbar bulges where tube strokes overlap.

Priorities: Integrate the lighting into a two-circuit marquee and give the face depth. Render each tube as nested passes over the lettering so the joins and fittings read cleanly.

## Changes made

- **156 · Silt** — Built continuous upper beds and a lower sequence offset at the fault. Narrowed the I's slab serifs, refit SILT, and reanchored both leaders.
- **157 · Quarter Turn** — Set the sweep on a track outside the graduations, redrew the arrow in the direction of travel, and connected the corner marks into a four-sided frame.
- **158 · The Quireline** — Redrew the thick–thin Q and its tail, staggered the leaf edges and sharpened the dog-ear, then opened the cover headline and aligned the story rules.
- **159 · Ink and Air** — Used shared rounded profiles for the solid and outlined B. Registered the numbered guides through the cleared counters and behind the isolated forms, and balanced the captions.
- **160 · Low Water** — Redrew the waterline and five tapered traces, removed the title underline, and reset the receding verse so the reversed phrase sits in clear water.
- **161 · Register 258** — Enlarged 258 and set a smaller 825 on the same cell axes. Made the cell boundaries visible and cut the stencils on a consistent diagonal.
- **162 · Oblique Assembly** — Refit LEAN, limited the construction guides to cap and baseline marks, and replaced the small wedge with overlaid upright and sheared stems showing a 30-unit run over a 100-unit rise.
- **163 · Three Voices** — Set progressively larger entries in reading order, carried each duration to a single release line, and replaced the tall dashed grid with short beat marks.
- **164 · The Fi Join** — Reused contours across the separate and joined forms, anchored the annotation leaders with dots, and opened the comparison around a drawn arrow.
- **165 · A Room After Dark** — Optically centered and kerned the tube letters, drew each glow pass across the whole word, and added a recessed bezel with alternating lamp circuits.

The set encoder now snaps polygon vertices to the coordinate grid before calculating relative offsets. This prevents long contours from accumulating rounding error, which showed up at the left edge of Low Water during the rebuild. The stream generators and validator already had no byte-size limit. Byte counts remain descriptive.

All ten rebuilt pages were reviewed at both sizes. The validator regenerated the streams, previews, contact sheet and manifest and passed the 7-bit, reset, palette, determinism and renderer checks, including the change of BLINK phase on page 165.

## Review of the current renders

This pass starts from the completed round-four outputs above. Each pair was inspected side by side at native size. The text readings below refer to 480 × 460.

### 156 · Silt

Cream against rust gives the display excellent contrast, and the custom bevels make the capitals feel cut from the section. SILT, “Sediment in relief,” “Exhibition in layers,” BED 01 and BED 02 all read. The I and L nearly bunch together while L–T opens a hole. At both sizes the thin layers are mechanically parallel, and the lower fault reads as a short diagonal scratch. The typographic cutaway is original and a good use of DRCS over native polygons, but the geology still looks like a stack of ribbons.

**Priorities.** Fit the visible letter edges. Vary bed thicknesses and carry a displaced marker seam through the fault so its movement reads.

### 157 · Quarter Turn

Lime and orchid are crisp against the black-violet field. Both QUARTERs, both TURNs and 90° read in their intended orientations. Native rotation gives the installation poster an original reason to be read from the edges. The flattened weight of the small ticks is particularly visible on the channel oval. The arrow points against the clockwise reading order, and the frame's patchy joins look like leftover crop marks.

**Priorities.** Make the selected quadrant and direction agree with the reading order. Clarify the graduations and reduce the frame to four clean edge indexes.

### 158 · The Quireline

The masthead, Issue 04, COVER STORY, “Inside the,” “paper fold,” “A journal of” and “folded matter” are legible. The cream and pink are convincing editorial colors. The nearly equal rules in the story column give each line the same emphasis. The Q's counter revealing the crease is an original use of a clear DRCS mask. At 640 the Q overhangs the book like a separate badge, while the repeated bottom edges turn into a dark comb at 480.

**Priorities.** Build the folded sheet and Q around the same axis. Group the story title as one unit and give the journal descriptor its own alignment.

### 159 · Ink and Air

INK, AIR, Printed B, Counterforms and both pairs of 1 and 2 read. The mint reversal and reuse of identical clear and solid counter masks make this one of the clearest and most original specimens. The numbers sit at opposing edges, so the eye has to infer which measurements match. The dashed lines disappear into the filled letter on the left and resume from the opposite side on the right. At 640 the outer outline's thin waist also looks fragile.

**Priorities.** Put a single pair of indices on the split and branch each guide toward the matching forms. Give the parent outline a cleaner waist and balance the caption margins.

### 160 · Low Water

“Low water,” “The tide takes,” “its blue back,” “one syllable” and “at a time.” all read. The pale-to-teal reversal makes the original concrete poem work as type, with native text and filled geometry sharing the composition. The first three lines look more center-stacked than receding. The shore comes uncomfortably close to “syllable” at 480, and the lower parallel tracks look like a second set of unrelated waves at 640.

**Priorities.** Pace the verse with a more pronounced horizontal stagger and clear the shore from the third line. Build the tidal traces from the same profile and let them thin around the last line.

### 161 · Register 258

“Register,” “Tabular,” 258, 825 and “Equal advance” are readable in amber and slate. The original DRCS stencil figures give the industrial specimen a purpose, but putting the smaller row on the same centers only establishes shared centers. The ghost uprights show through the 8 and read like defects at both sizes. The figures feel close to the rules above and below.

**Priorities.** Reuse the exact cell advances in both rows and describe that width with a common dimension rail. Keep center marks outside the figure silhouettes.

### 162 · Oblique Assembly

“Oblique Assembly,” LEAN, “Drawing,” “italic capitals,” “30 run” and “100 rise” read. The 30 and 100 are the tightest setting at channel size. Blue construction marks and lemon ground set off the native sheared polygons. The workshop premise is specific and original, but the right leg of A looks nearly vertical, and the tiny overlaid stems read as an icon with loose labels. The blue cap and baseline currently act more like borders than measurements.

**Priorities.** Give the A a real two-leg structure and improve the fit. Enlarge the shear diagram, directly attach its dimensions and relate it to a visible construction axis in the word.

### 163 · Three Voices

“Three Voices,” the three VOXs and “Unaccompanied voices” all read in cream, pink and lilac against burgundy. Scaling one original DRCS word is an economical expression of three related voices. The shared release makes sense, but the bare continuation rules and unrelated ticks make the central field look like a project timeline, especially at 640. The tiny separation between type and rule is inconsistent at 480.

**Priorities.** Put the beats on a common vertical grid. Give each sustained line an intentional shape and a consistent gap below the type.

### 164 · The Fi Join

“The fi join,” Hook, Join, the giant fi, the small f and i, arrow, small fi and “One stroke, two letters” read. Vermilion contrast is excellent and the custom hooked ligature is unusually specific to the theme. The dot near the hook can look stranded, and the long Join leader lays directly on top of the bar. The small joined setting is wider than the separated specimens' scale warrants, especially visible at 640. That weakens an otherwise original drawing plate.

**Priorities.** Show the separate and joined contours at exactly the same scale and on common guides. Route the join annotation away from the stroke and identify the point where the two drawings meet.

### 165 · A Room After Dark

“A room after dark,” OPEN, LATE and “Read until dawn” are clear. Peach against cobalt is strong enough to carry the poster. Palette BLINK and layered vector tubes exploit the medium, although the generic marquee is less original than the fitted lettering. The flat diagonal corners of O and P make the tubes look bent from strips, and the horizontal divider splits the layout into two equally heavy signs at both sizes.

**Priorities.** Form the tube bends as curves and give the two words a clearer hierarchy. Tie the animated circuits into the enclosure and cut back the central divider.

## Changes made in this pass

- **156 · Silt** — Refit the display capitals and drew variable-thickness laminae as dense polygon contours. Both fault blocks now meet on a continuous slip plane, with the pale marker bed visibly displaced.
- **157 · Quarter Turn** — Made the sweep clockwise, shaded its selected quadrant and introduced 48 graduations at three weights. Four centered edge marks replace the perimeter frame.
- **158 · The Quireline** — Built a folded sheet and return around the Q's axis. Simplified the exposed edges, tightened the story headline into one block and separated the journal descriptor.
- **159 · Ink and Air** — Set one numbered pair of guides on the panel seam, registering the matching forms at each height. Reshaped and strengthened the right parent outline.
- **160 · Low Water** — Staggered the receding lines more distinctly, lowered the shore and drew six traces from the same profile. Upper traces taper and break around the final line.
- **161 · Register 258** — Set 258 and 825 at identical size in equal cells, with a shared rail measuring each cell and its center ticks outside the figures.
- **162 · Oblique Assembly** — Redrew the legs and counter of A, showed the upright-to-oblique offset above N, and enlarged the lower inset with explicit run and rise brackets.
- **163 · Three Voices** — Gave the three entries tapered sustain marks ending together, organized the beat ticks on shared axes and left the letterforms and counters clear.
- **164 · The Fi Join** — Set separate and joined specimens at identical scale on matching construction lines. Moved the join leader away from the bar and onto the contour junction.
- **165 · A Room After Dark** — Rounded the tube bends with sampled curves, gave OPEN the larger setting and replaced the divider with three lamps. The side circuits now follow the bezel and alternate across it.
