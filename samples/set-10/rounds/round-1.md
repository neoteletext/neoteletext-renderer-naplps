# Round 1

The critique covers the existing channel-size render and the 640 × 480 render side by side. The text inventories describe the 480 × 460 render.

## Critique and priorities

### 156 · Silt

The cream and burnt-red contrast works, and the dark brown seam gives the page a clear bottom edge. The slab lettering has an original quarried character, but the wide gap before T and the jumping cap line make it feel like four objects dropped on a stock landscape. The broad flat polygons barely suggest sediment. “Sediment in relief,” “SILT,” and “An exhibition in layers” all read clearly, with the I squeezing in visibly between its neighbours. The design belongs on an exhibition poster. **Priorities**: optically space the DRCS word on a shared baseline and draw finer, related seams with room for the lettering.

### 157 · Quarter Turn

Lime and orchid are crisp against aubergine. All four native rotations work, and the pair of “QUARTER”s and pair of “TURN”s can be read in their respective orientations. The inset rectangle feels like a screen bezel. The loose pair of concentric circles and the conspicuously slashed zero make “90°” feel improvised, although it is readable. The rotation itself is an original fit for the installation poster. **Priorities**: build a dial with explicit quarter-turn geometry and draw a purpose-made central number with a clean zero.

### 158 · The Quireline

The pink and cream on violet have good contrast. “The Quireline,” “Issue 04,” “Inside the / paper fold,” and “A journal of / folded matter” all read, though the last two lines come close to the right margin. The mask is a good use of DRCS. The equal-spaced lines behind Q read as a ruled notebook, and its little foot looks accidental. The oversized initial is a strong cover idea with a familiar execution. **Priorities**: make the rule field behave like folded sheets and draw a more resolved Q with a tapered tail.

### 159 · Ink and Air

The mint reversal gives both panels excellent contrast. “INK,” “AIR,” both Bs, “Stroke,” and “Counter” are all legible. The flush split and matched rules are orderly, but the second impression explains little about the shapes of the counters. The overlapping boxes and ovals leave visible steps on the B, especially where the bowls meet the spine. It fits a specimen, but the mirror treatment is familiar. **Priorities**: redraw continuous contours and use the second panel to isolate the two counterforms.

### 160 · Low Water

The pale bank and dark teal give every line enough contrast. “Low water,” “The tide takes,” “its blue back,” “one syllable,” and “at a time.” read, though the last phrase has an unexpectedly large vertical gap. The stepped proportional setting belongs in concrete poetry, but the rigid diagonal and stripes make the tide resemble a racetrack. **Priorities**: give the shoreline a more deliberate contour and carry the final phrase into it with a steady reading cadence.

### 161 · Register 258

Amber on navy is clear. “Register,” “Tabular,” “258,” and “Stencilled figures” read. The strong cell alignment shows NAPLPS fixed-width fields, but the digits borrow the familiar seven-segment look. Their diagonal gaps and centre slot compete, and several segment corners almost touch. The panel resembles a counter without quite working as a type specimen. **Priorities**: draw original fitted numeral outlines with deliberate stencil bridges and expose the tabular spacing with shared register marks.

### 162 · Oblique Assembly

Lemon, ink and blue give the poster energy. “Oblique Assembly,” “LEAN,” and “Drawing italic capitals” all read; the A has the tightest counter. The sheared polygons suit the workshop theme and exploit the vector grid. The bent blue stripes have no clear relationship to the letter angle, and the A and N run close at their feet. The resulting sports-poster composition is familiar. **Priorities**: use oblique construction guides that share the shear of the letters and open up the A and its spacing.

### 163 · Three Voices

Cream, pink and lilac each read against the oxide field. “Three Voices,” each of the three “VOX” settings, and “For unaccompanied voices” are legible, although the footer nearly fills its rule. Reusable DRCS lettering works, but the equal settings and regular staircase imply three identical voices. The little waveforms look like stock audio symbols. It suits a programme cover, with room for a more original typographic score. **Priorities**: vary the register and width of the entries and make their entry lines part of one common score.

### 164 · The Fi Join

Vermilion against ivory makes the ligature dominant. “The fi join,” the enlarged “fi,” “Hook,” “Join,” and “One stroke, two letters” read. The single DRCS cut is an original use of the format, but the polygon kinks around the hook and the leaders touch the ink, leaving muddy dots. The drawing plate tells us where to look without showing how the join changes the forms. **Priorities**: refine the hook and join contour and replace the on-ink dots with external drafting leaders and a small repeat of the ligature.

### 165 · A Room After Dark

Apricot on blue reads well. “A room after dark,” “OPEN,” “LATE,” and “Read until dawn” are clear. The upside-down “LATE” can be decoded, though it reads as stray backward letters in the upright orientation and its low contrast slows it further. The native lettering and palette animation suit a nocturnal sign, but the frame looks generic and the single-word echo feels detached. **Priorities**: compose the words as a two-tier sign with a shared axis and use the palette phase to animate the sign's framing while keeping the copy steady.

## Changes made

- **156 · Silt** — Aligned the DRCS capitals on a common baseline, tightened the L–T spacing and built two finer contour seams into the rust band.
- **157 · Quarter Turn** — Kept the four reading orientations and rebuilt the centre as a graduated dial with a 90-degree arrow and custom DRCS numerals.
- **158 · The Quireline** — Replaced the notebook rules with a folded sheet and redrew the Q, removing the foot and tapering the tail. The fold is visible through the counter.
- **159 · Ink and Air** — Rebuilt the B with a continuous outline and reused its counter contours as isolated positive shapes on the mint panel at the same scale.
- **160 · Low Water** — Reset the verse with more even intervals, bent the shore around the descending lines and moved the water contours below the reversed final phrase.
- **161 · Register 258** — Replaced seven-segment figures with three custom stencil outlines and added repeated ticks at the cell edges and centres.
- **162 · Oblique Assembly** — Anchored the lettering to cap and baseline guides with matching slanted construction lines. Adjusted the glyph positions and opened the A counter.
- **163 · Three Voices** — Set VOX at three widths and heights on a shared score, with ticks that mark the entrances in place of the waveforms.
- **164 · The Fi Join** — Refined the hook and join, moved the leaders off the ink and added two reduced impressions for comparison.
- **165 · A Room After Dark** — Aligned OPEN and LATE inside a chamfered marquee and transferred the palette cycle from the inverted echo to the inner frame and diamonds.
