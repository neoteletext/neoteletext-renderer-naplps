# Round 2 · Typography

I reviewed the incoming 640 × 480 and 480 × 460 renders side by side at native size. The channel render is narrower and makes spacing and small joins easier to judge. The legibility notes below cover the channel render.

## 156 · Silt

The sand and rust have excellent contrast and the band gives the page a clear silhouette. The header and footer feel like detached labels, and the repeated edges of the band look more like a ribbon than a geological section. SILT, “Sediment in relief” and “An exhibition in layers” all read clearly. The cut DRCS letters suit an exhibition poster, but the square T looks borrowed from a more ordinary face, and the I–L gap pinches relative to L–T. The premise is more distinctive than the current drawing.

**Priorities.** Recut the T and fit the four letters optically. Draw strata with internal lenses and a break in the lower beds, and use a numbered section key to give the framing copy a role.

## 157 · Quarter Turn

Lime and orchid separate the reading directions cleanly. Both copies of QUARTER and TURN remain readable when mentally rotated, and 90° is clear. The rotation is integral to the poster and unusually apt for the medium. The flat inset looks like a backing board, and the faint tick ring and floating arrow only partly explain the movement. The broken relation between ring and pointer feels unfinished at both sizes, with a more elongated dial at channel size.

**Priorities.** Make the dial and arc share the same radial construction. Give the four rotated readings clear corner registers and take up the dead space around the inset.

## 158 · The Quireline

Cream and pink have good contrast against violet. “The Quireline”, “Issue 04”, “Inside the”, “paper fold”, “A journal of” and “folded matter” are all readable, though the copy crowds the right edge. The oversized Q makes a convincing cover initial and its clear DRCS counter is well used. The repeated zigzags flatten the folded sheet into a ruled texture, and the huge right-hand gap between headline and descriptor feels unedited. The fold concept has some originality, but the cover lacks the depth and hierarchy it promises.

**Priorities.** Draw planes and exposed leaf edges so the fold reads through the Q. Rebuild the right column with a clear hierarchy and breathing room at the outer edge.

## 159 · Ink and Air

The inverse green panels are the strongest colour relationship in the set. INK, AIR, B, Stroke and Counters are clear. The extracted shapes are deliberately unlettered and retain the proportions of the B counters. Reusing identical DRCS outlines for subtraction and printing is a credible and original specimen device. The specimens have a slight horizontal registration error, and guides brush the holes on the right in a way that makes them resemble a chart.

**Priorities.** Register the two glyph cells precisely. Carry a shared system of matching counter guides and numbered annotations across the diptych.

## 160 · Low Water

The title and all four lines (“The tide takes”, “its blue back”, “one syllable”, “at a time.”) read. The last line has enough contrast, but sits cramped between shoreline and contour lines. The blue-green reversal is apt. The original verse and stepped setting belong to concrete typography, though the abrupt last indent and three blunt parallel ends make it look like a caption laid over a map. Native proportional text and reversal do the work; geometry could participate more directly.

**Priorities.** Give the verse a deliberate progression in scale and spacing with a clear landing for the reversal. Draw separate, tapering tidal traces that follow the word rhythm.

## 161 · Register 258

“Register”, “Tabular”, 258 and “Stencilled figures” are readable. Amber on blue feels appropriate, and the equal cell advances make this recognisably a numeral specimen. The stencil breaks look like three unrelated accidents, especially where the 5 appears cracked across its spine. The center ticks describe a grid but offer little evidence of tabularity. The mechanical premise is familiar; the original numerals are the opportunity.

**Priorities.** Coordinate the stencil bridges and chamfers across all three numerals. Show a reordered line in the same columns and make the cell centers do explanatory work.

## 162 · Oblique Assembly

“Oblique Assembly”, LEAN and “Drawing italic capitals” are clear. The hard lemon and cobalt contrast makes a convincing workshop poster. A visibly sheared vector face is a strong use of NAPLPS geometry, but the four near-identical guides largely disappear against the letters, the A looks crowded at its apex and the poster doesn't really show how the shear works. The phrasing and display word have wit; the construction is rudimentary.

**Priorities.** Make a true upright reference and run/rise diagram tied to the displayed shear. Open and space the A so its inner triangle survives the channel rendering.

## 163 · Three Voices

The cream, pink and lilac all read against oxide. “Three Voices”, all three VOX entries and “For unaccompanied voices” are clear. The successive entries are a suitable compositional idea, but widening the repeat faster than it grows taller makes the lowest word look flattened, and the stub marks float away from the entrances. Matching right ends produce a stack of logos. The repeated DRCS word could act more convincingly as three voices in a score.

**Priorities.** Keep consistent glyph proportions while varying scale. Put the three entrances on a common time grid with guide marks that actually meet each onset.

## 164 · The Fi Join

Ivory, warm grey and vermilion make a convincing drawing plate. “The fi join”, “Hook”, “Join” and “One stroke, two letters” read. All three red cuts are visible, though the smallest is difficult to identify in isolation and none has an i-dot, so the display resembles an f fused to an n or an undotted i. The custom DRCS makes the page genuinely original, but the leaders almost point to the right places and the tiny samples feel squeezed into leftover space.

**Priorities.** Draw a clear i-dot and fit the terminal and join around it. Give leaders specific targets and set the ligature in a readable word at specimen size.

## 165 · A Room After Dark

“A room after dark”, OPEN, LATE and “Read until dawn” are all clear. The peach on cobalt is inviting and BLINK suits a night sign. The fixed-pitch display looks mechanically spaced, and the large double frame and diamonds could belong to any marquee. The concept is on theme, but most of the personality currently comes from the colours. At both sizes the resting border phase looks dull.

**Priorities.** Fit a custom inline display face by eye. Rework the hardware around a small set of lamp details and make the illumination pulse without changing the letter bodies.

## Changes made

- **156 · Silt** — Recut the T with a chamfered terminal and refit SILT. Drew two sediment lenses, offset the lower beds across a fault, and keyed the framing lines with BED 01 and BED 02.
- **157 · Quarter Turn** — Replaced the inset with corner registers. Drew the tick ring and highlighted quarter arc on the same radius with a start pin and directional arrowhead.
- **158 · The Quireline** — Modelled the fold with contrasting planes, a turned corner and exposed leaf edges visible around and through the Q. Reset the right column under a COVER STORY label.
- **159 · Ink and Air** — Registered the B and its extracted counters on identical cells translated by half a page. Paired the two shapes with horizontal guides and matching 1 and 2 labels.
- **160 · Low Water** — Stepped the first three verse lines down in scale and let the final reversal sit in a clear field of blue. Replaced the parallel outlines with separate, tapering tidal traces.
- **161 · Register 258** — Aligned stencil breaks across the feet of the numerals, coordinated outer chamfers, and set 825 below 258 to show the figures trading places on the same centerlines.
- **162 · Oblique Assembly** — Opened the A counter and adjusted its spacing. Added a diagram with a vertical reference and 30 run / 100 rise labels calculated from the displayed shear.
- **163 · Three Voices** — Put the three VOX entrances on a shared time grid, aligned the onset marks with the V, and scaled each word at the same glyph proportions.
- **164 · The Fi Join** — Dotted the i, anchored the leaders to the hook and join, and paired the smaller ligature with a matching DRCS t to set “fit”.
- **165 · A Room After Dark** — Built fitted OPEN and LATE capitals from shadow, face and inline strokes. Replaced the inner border with lamp runs and moved the palette cycle to the lamps.

## Verification

Regenerated all ten streams, twenty PNGs, the contact sheet and the manifest with `validate.py`. All ten streams passed determinism, 7-bit, full reset, initial colour map and renderer diagnostics. The byte ceiling was already removed in Round 1. The generator and validator audit found only informational byte counts. I reviewed the revised renders at both resolutions, including the new labels at 480 × 460. Pixel comparison between the two marquee phases confirmed that only the lamp colour changed at both sizes.
